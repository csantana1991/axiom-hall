[[BEAT 0]]

Name the idea in the blunt form you can use under pressure. A future mid in the feature is not a feature. Label construction is where leakage hides. If the label is the next return and the feature uses a window centered on now, half of the window is the label's accomplice. Centered smoothers are guilty until proven causal. Any input not knowable at decision time counts, and that includes a scaler fit on the full sample and a vocabulary built with the test set.

The label is allowed to see the future. That is the point of a label. The next return is a function of a later price. You will compute it when you build the training table, and you will compute it when you score a forecast after the fact. The line you may not cross is the line between that label and the inputs that were supposed to be known when the decision was made. Leakage is what you get when the later price takes a side door into those inputs. The side door is often a smoother with a pleasant name, a normalization you applied once to the whole file, or a dictionary of categories you built before you cut the sample.

Work a centered window on prices that make the accomplice visible. This path is an illustration, not the lab. The mids at times zero through four are ten, ten, ten, twelve, and twelve. Put now at time two, so the current mid is ten. The next mid, at time three, is twelve. The next simple return is twelve divided by ten, minus one, which is one fifth. A window of width five centered on now uses times zero, one, two, three, and four. Two of those five times are strictly after now. The lecture's short sentence calls half the window the accomplice. On this count the strictly future portion is two fifths, and one of those two future mids is exactly the mid inside the label. The average of the five mids is ten plus ten plus ten plus twelve plus twelve, which is fifty-four, divided by five, which is ten and four fifths. The current mid is ten. The smoothed feature sits four fifths of a point above the current mid, and both of those four fifths came from the two future twelves. Ten and four fifths minus ten is four fifths, and four fifths divided by two future steps is two fifths of a point of future mid in each of those steps, which matches the jump from ten to twelve. You did not need a subtle statistic. The feature moved because the label's price moved.

A causal window on the same clock does not move. Take times zero, one, and two only, the three mids knowable at time two if the mid at two is known at the decision. Their average is ten. Subtract the current mid and you get zero. The jump to twelve is invisible, which is what you want in a feature, because that jump is the label. Prove the smoother was causal, or do not use it. A picture that looks smooth because it averaged tomorrow into today is not a denoised version of today. It is tomorrow, diluted.

Change the label's timing and watch the same window commit a slightly different theft. Suppose the label is not the one-step return but the two-step return, from the mid at time two to the mid at time four. Time four is twelve, time two is ten, and the simple return is again one fifth. The centered window still contains time three and time four. Time four is the end of the label. Time three is the path in between. Both are unknowable at time two. If your defense is that you did not use the next mid, only a nearby mid, time three is the next mid and time four is the one after. You used both. Centered smoothers are guilty until proven causal means you do not get to start from the assumption that a symmetric filter is harmless and then wait for someone to prosecute it. You start from the assumption that it reads the future, and you replace it with a window whose last timestamp is the decision time.

The scaler is the same crime in a different uniform. Suppose the raw feature on three training days is zero, two, and four, and on two test days it is ten and twelve. This is a constructed file, so you can see the mean move. The training-only mean is zero plus two plus four, divided by three, which is two. A training-only centering subtracts two. The training values become negative two, zero, and two. The test values, centered with the same training mean, become eight and ten. You have not used the test days to define the transformation. Now fit the center on the full sample, which is the mistake. The five numbers sum to twenty-eight. The mean is twenty-eight fifths, or five and three fifths. Subtract that from the training value four and you get four minus five and three fifths, which is negative one and three fifths. The same recorded feature, on a training day, has changed from plus two under an honest center to negative one and three fifths under a leaky center. The test values ten and twelve pulled the origin up by three and three fifths, and every training row slid with it. The model is allowed to see a version of the past that already knows the future average. A scaler fit on the full sample is leakage even when the formula for the scaler is printed in a textbook and feels like preprocessing rather than like a feature.

Do the vocabulary version with the same strictness, because it does not look like a price. Suppose the training days only ever produce two imbalance bins, call them low and high, and a test day produces a third bin, call it extreme. A vocabulary built on the training days has two entries. The test day is out of that vocabulary, and you need a rule you froze in advance for unseen bins, such as a single unknown bucket that was already available before the test day existed. A vocabulary built with the test set has three entries. The extreme bin receives its own coordinate because you looked at the test day. That coordinate is an input that was not knowable as a named direction in the feature space at training time, and it is not knowable at a decision time that precedes the test day. Includes a vocabulary built with the test set means exactly this. The dictionary is part of the model. If the dictionary was compiled with the future, the model was compiled with the future.

A lag is the same test with a friendlier reputation, so count it on the same mids. The mids were ten, ten, ten, twelve, and twelve at times zero through four, and now is time two. A feature defined as the mid one step ahead is twelve, the mid at time three. That twelve is not a lag. A lag looks backward. The mid at time one, which is ten, is a lag of one step and is knowable at time two if you froze that rule. The mid at time three is a lead, and a lead of the thing inside the label is the label's accomplice with the sign reversed. People say lag when they mean "a shift," and a shift of the wrong sign is leakage. Write the sign. If the code says shift by minus one and your convention is that negative looks forward, you have built a lead and named it a lag. The timestamp test does not care what the function was called. It cares that time three is after time two. Ten is legal. Twelve is not. The centered average of ten and four fifths was illegal because it mixed the legal tens with that twelve and the twelve after it. Removing the name "smooth" and writing "lead" does not repair it, and writing "lag" on a negative shift does not repair it either.

One more construction, because label alignment is where this hides even when every column looks old. Suppose the feature row is stamped at time two and truly uses only times zero, one, and two. The label beside it in the file was computed at time one, from the jump that ends at time two. Then the feature's current mid, ten at time two, is inside the previous label, and you have aligned a feature with a label that already contains it. The row looks causal if you only inspect the feature code. It is not causal under the join you actually trained. Write the timestamp of the feature and the timestamp of the decision the label belongs to. If the feature timestamp is later than the decision timestamp, the join is the leak. This is still label construction, not a new category. It is the reason a column of clean lags can be guilty after a bad merge.

The question that comes up is whether a centered smoother is harmless when you use it only to draw a chart, and the fit itself is causal. A chart that is not an input is not a feature. You may draw it, and you should label it as a view that uses the future, so nobody reads the smoothness as something a trader could have seen. The moment a number from that chart enters the design, even as a reason you chose a window, you are back in the leakage. "I only used it to decide the width, and then I refit causally" is a use. The width is then a function of the future chart. If the width is chosen on the training block alone, with a causal smoother, the objection expires. Guilty until proven causal is a placement of the burden, not a ban on averages.

What the claim does not mean is that every feature which correlates with the next return is leaked. A legal feature can still move with the label by ordinary dependence. Dependence is not a side door. The side door is a timestamp. If every input timestamp is at or before the decision, and every fitting choice that touches the inputs was made without the test block, a high correlation is a result you may then fail to trust for other reasons. It is not automatically leakage. The claim also does not mean that the label should be censored so that it cannot see the next mid. Starve the label and you have no supervision. Starve the feature of the next mid and you have a chance of an honest one.

[[BEAT 1]]

The idea that replaces the random cut is walk-forward. Train on the half-open interval from T zero to T one, test on the half-open interval from T one to T two, and roll forward. Do not tune on the test window and then report it. The purge and the embargo around the cut exist because labels overlap the boundary. López de Prado's work is the long version. The short version is that the cut is part of the model. One honest window beats twelve tuned ones.

Read the intervals as inequalities so the endpoint cannot smuggle a day across the cut. A day t is in training when T zero is less than or equal to t and t is strictly less than T one. A day t is in test when T one is less than or equal to t and t is strictly less than T two. The test starts where the training ends. There is no shared day. Roll forward means the next experiment builds a later pair, with a later T one and a later T two, and the previous test block is not quietly promoted into a place where you mine it for the story you tell about the new test block. You may, in a later roll, train on days that were once future relative to an earlier cut. That is allowed because, at the later decision, those days are past. It is not allowed to use them as past before they were past.

Put numbers on one cut. Let T zero be day zero, T one be day one hundred, and T two be day one hundred twenty. Training holds one hundred days, indexes zero through ninety-nine. Testing holds twenty days, indexes one hundred through one hundred nineteen. Nothing about these lengths is sacred. They are a worked case so the embargo has something to bite. Suppose the label horizon is five days. A decision on day ninety-six has a label that looks five days ahead, through day one hundred one. Day one hundred and day one hundred one sit in the test interval. The training row for day ninety-six is therefore built with labels that overlap the test block. The fit sees a function of test-period prices, attached to a row you called training. That is the overlap the purge and the embargo are there to break.

Embargo at least that horizon means the gap you refuse to learn across is at least five days in this example, not a token one-day skip you hope is polite. One concrete way to honor the sentence is to stop training decisions at day one hundred minus five, so the last training decision is on day ninety-five, and its label, reaching five days ahead, ends on day one hundred, which is the first test day. Whether the end is inclusive depends on how you defined the horizon, so write the horizon down before you subtract. If the label uses prices strictly after the decision and up through five days after, a decision on day ninety-five touches day one hundred. If day one hundred is test, you have still touched it, and the embargo has to move one day earlier, to day ninety-four, whose five-day reach ends at day ninety-nine, the last training day. The arithmetic is fussy because the boundary is fussy. The fuss is the model. A person who writes "embargo of five" and then cannot say whether day ninety-five is in or out has not chosen an embargo. They have chosen a word.

Roll the same design forward once, so the verb "roll" is not decorative. The next cut takes T zero still at day zero, or at some later restart you declare, T one at day one hundred twenty, and T two at day one hundred forty. Test is now days one hundred twenty through one hundred thirty-nine. The old test days, one hundred through one hundred nineteen, can enter training for this new cut, because they are past relative to day one hundred twenty. They cannot enter the score you already reported for the first cut. That score was sealed when you looked at it, or it was not a test. And the embargo moves with the cut. The last legal training decision is again at least a horizon before day one hundred twenty. You recompute the subtraction at the new boundary. You do not copy the old row indexes and feel that the embargo is a property of the dataset rather than of the cut.

Here is the twelve-window failure, counted rather than scolded. Suppose you build twelve walk-forward tests, each honest in isolation, and each produces a score. You look at the twelve scores and you put the largest one in the abstract. You have used the test windows as a search space. There were twelve chances to be pleased, and you reported the pleasure. One honest window beats twelve tuned ones means you would rather publish a single predeclared cut than a maximum over a dozen cuts you ranked after seeing them. If you want twelve cuts, report the twelve, or report a rule for aggregating them that you wrote before you saw them. The maximum is a different statistic. It is biased upward by the selection, and the selection is tuning.

A small version makes the bias easy to see without a probability lecture. Suppose the twelve scores, as an illustration and not as a lab result, are zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, and one. Eleven windows say zero. One window says one. The honest description is that the predeclared window, whichever it was, has its own number, and that the set of twelve contains a one. The tuned description says the method scored one. Those sentences describe different procedures. The cut is part of the model because changing which window you are willing to call "the" result changes the procedure. López de Prado's work is the long version of purge, embargo, and the overlapping-label problem. You are not being asked, in this room, to recite the long version from memory. You are being asked to keep the short version in the fit: the cut is part of the model, and a label that crosses it is not a training label.

Do the subtraction on a horizon that is not five, so the number five does not become a superstition. Let the cut stay at day one hundred, and let the label horizon be one day, where the label uses the next day's price and no further. A decision on day ninety-nine has a label that touches day one hundred. Day one hundred is the first test day. If touching day one hundred is forbidden, the last training decision is day ninety-eight, whose next day is ninety-nine, still inside training. The embargo is at least one day, and the off-by-one of the endpoint can make it two steps of index, from ninety-nine down to ninety-eight, depending on whether the horizon's last day is inclusive. Write that down. A ten-day horizon on the same cut pushes the last safe decision much earlier. If the label on day t uses days t plus one through t plus ten, then t plus ten must be at most ninety-nine, so t is at most eighty-nine. Day eighty-nine's label ends on day ninety-nine. Day ninety's label ends on day one hundred, which is test, so day ninety is out. The excluded training decisions are those t where t plus the horizon reaches the test start. With horizon ten and test start one hundred, t plus ten is at least one hundred when t is at least ninety. Inside a training region that would otherwise have ended at ninety-nine, the excluded decisions are ninety through ninety-nine. That is ten decisions. The embargo is at least the horizon, and under this endpoint convention the horizon and the number of excluded edge decisions match. They will not match under every convention, which is why you write the convention rather than memorizing ten.

If you roll twelve cuts and the embargo is wrong on each of them, you do not have twelve honest windows with a selection problem. You have twelve leaked windows, and selecting the best of them stacks a second failure on the first. Repair the embargo before you talk about honesty across windows. One clean cut with the ten edge decisions removed is the object the short version is pointing at. Twelve pretty numbers with the edge left in are not made honest by being numerous.

The question to answer is how long the embargo has to be. At least the label horizon. Not at most the horizon, and not a fixed courtesy gap that ignores the horizon you actually used. If the label looks ten days out, an embargo of five is short, and the rows near the cut still contain the test. If the label looks one day out, an embargo of one day is the minimum the sentence allows, and a longer embargo is a further choice you should name rather than a contradiction. If two labels are in the same model, with horizons five and ten, the embargo is at least the longer one, otherwise the longer label still reaches across. Write the horizon next to the cut. Then the subtraction is determined. If you cannot point to the horizon, you cannot point to the embargo, and you are not ready to report the test number.

What the claim does not mean is that a walk-forward split makes the model good, or that an embargo repairs a future mid hiding inside a feature far from the boundary. The split orders the blocks. The embargo protects the boundary against labels that hang off the edge of a block. A feature that reads tomorrow's mid on every row is leaked in the middle of the training block, where no embargo is looking. You still have to pass the timestamp test from the previous idea. The claim also does not mean that one window is statistically precise. One window can be noisy. The lecture prefers one honest window to twelve tuned ones because honesty and selection bias are the subject, not because a short test has a small variance. You may say the window is thin. You may not thicken it by picking the best of twelve and speaking as if you had only ever planned to run that one.

[[BEAT 2]]

The idea that keeps the lab from being misquoted is a negative one. You should be able to say what the attention lab is not. It is not a trained transformer, and it does not predict returns. The readout is a weighted sum of the past values, which on this toy equals a smoothed copy of the past. If someone quotes a Sharpe ratio from it, they are joking or they are wrong. The lab has no split because it has no fit. Turn the mask off in your head, not in the lab. Future weights would light up, and the exercise would be cheating. Keep the mask. Write the walk-forward cut you would have used if these six numbers had been a week.

A smoothed copy of the past is a specific object. Take three past values, two, zero, and two, and three weights that sum to one, one half, one quarter, and one quarter. The weighted sum is one half times two, plus one quarter times zero, plus one quarter times two, which is one plus zero plus one half, which is one and one half. Every input to that sum is a past value. The output one and one half sits between them. It does not claim that the next value will be one and one half. If the next value is nine, the smoother is not "wrong" about nine, because it never spoke about nine. It spoke about a weighted view of two, zero, and two. Calling that view a return forecast adds a verb the calculation does not contain.

Flatten the same three values to equal weights and the smoother becomes the ordinary mean. Two plus zero plus two is four, divided by three, which is four thirds. Four thirds is still a function of the past alone. Raising a temperature until the weights nearly match is still not a forecast. It is a more even copy. The lab's six signed imbalance numbers, if you attend causally and you set the value equal to the number itself, produce a number in the same units as those imbalances. A signed imbalance is not a return. Averaging it does not change its unit into a profit.

The checklist before any fit, for the day you do have a fit, is short enough to say in one breath and strict enough to fail a project. Timestamp on every column. Scaler fit on the training block only. Label horizon written down. Embargo at least that horizon. Costs in the score. A baseline that is embarrassingly simple, such as predict zero. If the model cannot beat predict zero after costs, the attention was a picture. The lab you are looking at today does not get to skip the spirit of that list by being small. It gets to skip the split only because there is no fit to split. The moment you fit, the list turns on.

Predict zero needs a loss so it does not stay a slogan, and so cancellation cannot masquerade as skill. Suppose the label is a simple return, and the five test labels, as an illustration, are one hundredth, negative two hundredths, one hundredth, three hundredths, and negative one hundredth. Predict zero. The squared errors are one ten-thousandth, four ten-thousandths, one ten-thousandth, nine ten-thousandths, and one ten-thousandth. The sum is sixteen ten-thousandths, which is zero point zero zero one six. The mean squared error is that sum divided by five, which is zero point zero zero zero three two. That number is the hurdle on this illustration. A model with a larger mean squared error has not beaten predict zero before costs. A model with a smaller one has beaten the baseline only before costs.

Put a cost in the score so the second half of the sentence is doing work. Suppose you froze a cost of one hundredth on every test decision, in the same units as the return, and you have five decisions. This cost is a supposition for the arithmetic, not a measured fee. Five times one hundredth is five hundredths. Suppose the model's summed signed edge on the five decisions, before cost, is three hundredths. After cost, three hundredths minus five hundredths is negative two hundredths. The model beat predict zero on a raw squared-error story you might tell, or it might not, but on this costed sum it is behind doing nothing, if doing nothing pays no cost and earns no edge. The checklist does not let you report the three hundredths and mention the cost in a footnote you do not subtract. Costs in the score means the number you quote is after the subtraction. If the model cannot beat predict zero after costs, the attention was a picture. A picture can be a correct picture of a weighted average and still be a picture rather than a trade.

Write the cut you would have used if the six lab numbers had been a week, even though the lab has no fit and therefore no split to run. Six days, indexes zero through five. Declare T zero at day zero, T one at day four, and T two at day six. Training would be days zero, one, two, and three. Test would be days four and five. Suppose the label horizon is one day, and the label on a decision uses the next day's move. The decision on day three, the last training day, has a label that uses day four, and day four is test. Embargo at least that horizon drops day three from training, and it drops any earlier day whose label still reaches day four. With a one-day horizon, day three is the overlapping decision, so the last kept training decision is day two, whose next day is day three, still inside training. Test remains days four and five. You have just written a cut, an embargo, and a horizon. You have not fit a model. The lab still has no split, because a split you would have used is not a split you ran. Writing it is the exercise. Reporting a performance number on days four and five, from a weighted average that was not a forecast, would still be a joke.

Apply the hurdle to a model number so the baseline is a comparison and not only a definition. The predict-zero mean squared error on the illustration was zero point zero zero zero three two. Suppose a candidate forecast, still an illustration, produces squared errors whose mean is zero point zero zero zero two. That is smaller than zero point zero zero zero three two, so on squared error, before costs, the candidate beats predict zero. The margin is zero point zero zero zero three two minus zero point zero zero zero two, which is zero point zero zero zero one two. Now put the same one-hundredth cost on each of the five decisions, five hundredths in total, against a summed signed edge of three hundredths. The costed result is still negative two hundredths. Beating the squared-error hurdle did not survive the cost. The checklist is a conjunction. Timestamps, a training-only scaler, a written horizon, an embargo at least that horizon, costs inside the score, and a win over predict zero after those costs. Fail the cost and the attention was a picture, even if the squared error looked better than guessing a flat zero.

If these six lab numbers were a week and you also had a label, the cut you wrote, train through day two after the embargo, test on days four and five, is where that comparison would have to live. You would score predict zero on days four and five only, after costs, and you would not tune the temperature on those two days and then quote them. The lab does not give you the label. Writing the cut is not permission to invent the label's Sharpe ratio. It is permission to see where a Sharpe ratio would have been illegal.

The question to close is whether careful wording could turn that smoothed copy into a Sharpe ratio. No. If someone quotes a Sharpe ratio from this lab, they are joking or they are wrong. A Sharpe ratio is a claim about excess return over a scale of volatility, and this lab does not predict returns. It does not have a position, a costed profit, or a clock of trades. The readout is a smoothed copy of past toy imbalances. You can compute that copy by hand. You cannot divide it by a volatility and obtain a performance statistic, because you never formed the excess return the ratio needs. Careful wording does not help. The missing object is the return, not the adjective.

What the claim does not mean is that attention is useless, or that a later model which does predict returns is forbidden to use a causal weighted average as one of its inputs. The prohibition is on this lab, and on any quote that treats the lab as a backtest. A later model faces the full checklist: timestamps, a training-only scaler, a written horizon, an embargo at least that long, costs inside the score, and a predict-zero baseline it has to beat after those costs. If it fails that list, the attention inside it was a picture, even if the surrounding network was large. The claim also does not mean that keeping the mask is optional once the story is causal in your head. Turn the mask off in your head so you can see that future weights would light up. Keep it on in the lab. The head version is how you understand the cheat. The lab version is how you refuse to commit it.

When you leave, you should be able to say four negatives without looking down. The lab is not a transformer you trained. It is not a return model. It is not a split, because it is not a fit. It is not a source of a Sharpe ratio. What it is, is a weighted sum you can recompute, under a mask, on six numbers, with a cut written beside it in case those six numbers are ever promoted into a week. Promotion is a new experiment. It is not a reinterpretation of the picture you already have.
