The idea in this opening block is that the spread pays someone for a hazard. A market maker earns the spread when the flow is uninformed, and loses to informed flow that picks off a stale quote. The posted spread is a price for that mixture. That is Glosten and Milgrom's story, stripped to one paragraph. The lab does not simulate informed traders. It only gives you the state variables the story uses.

Say the two sides of the mixture before you touch a number. Uninformed flow, in this story, is flow that does not know something the quote has missed. The maker who sells at the ask, or buys at the bid, collects the distance between the trade and the mid, and the mid is the summary the notes use as a reference. Informed flow is the other piece. It trades because the quote is stale relative to something the maker has not yet put into the price. The maker then loses on the inventory that the informed trade left behind. The posted spread is not a receipt for one of those pieces. It is a price for the mixture of both. If you tell the story, you have to say which piece you are talking about, because the lecture separates them.

The slide names the two risks in plain language, and you should keep the names distinct. Inventory risk is that you hold a position you did not want. Selection risk is that the flow you traded was informed. Spreads can compensate either. Identify which one you are telling. A sentence that says "the spread compensates the maker" without naming inventory or selection has not yet said which story is on the table. The lab will not choose for you. It does not contain a simulated informed trader. When you look at the ladder, you are looking at bids, asks, and sizes, which are the state variables the story uses, not a draw from the mixture.

Now compute the touch the notes actually give you. The touch is ninety-nine point nine eight against one hundred point zero two. Do the subtraction in cents so the borrowing is visible. Ninety-nine point nine eight dollars is nine thousand nine hundred ninety-eight cents. One hundred point zero two dollars is ten thousand and two cents. Ten thousand two minus nine thousand nine hundred ninety-eight is four. The spread is four cents. Displayed as dollars, that is zero point zero four. You can check the addition the other way. Ninety-nine point nine eight plus two cents is one hundred even. One hundred even plus two cents is one hundred point zero two. The two additions of two cents are the four-cent spread, split evenly only because these two prices happen to sit two cents on each side of one hundred.

The mid is the average of the best bid and the best ask. Add the cent counts first. Nine thousand nine hundred ninety-eight plus ten thousand two is twenty thousand. Divide by two and you get ten thousand cents, which displays as one hundred point zero zero dollars. The notes call this the decision mid in the worked shortfall, and on this touch the decision mid and the touch mid are the same number. Half of the four-cent spread is two cents. The ask sits two cents above the mid. The bid sits two cents below the mid. If uninformed flow lifts the offer, the maker who sold at one hundred point zero two collected two cents a share relative to that mid, before fees and before any later move. That two cents is the earning side of the story, and it is only the earning side. The lab does not then move the price to show the informed side. You do not get to watch the loss. You only get the state the loss would be marked against.

The original touch was special. Its spread was an even number of cents and its mid landed on a cent. You might ask whether the spread is the market maker's profit. It is not. The posted spread is a price for a mixture. You earn the distance to the mid when the flow is uninformed. You lose to informed flow that picks off a stale quote. Inventory is a second way the same posted number can be compensation, and it is a different sentence: you were paid to hold a position you did not want. On the original touch, the two cents from mid to ask is the distance an uninformed lift would hand the maker, before fees. It is not a profit figure, because the story's other branch has not been added, and because the lab never runs that branch. If you report two cents as profit, you have dropped both the mixture and the fees the later note tells you to keep in view.

What the claim does not mean is a list you should be able to refuse out loud. It does not mean every spread you see is compensation for adverse selection. It might be inventory, and the slide tells you to identify which one you are telling. It does not mean the lab contains informed traders. The lecture says the opposite: the lab does not simulate them. It does not mean the four-cent spread is a measured mixture weight. Nothing in the touch tells you what fraction of flow is informed. It does not mean the mid is a trade. The mid is an average of two resting prices. No one has crossed. It does not mean a five-cent spread, in the changed touch, is a worse market in some moral sense. It is a wider hole, and the mid fell between cents. That is arithmetic. It does not mean you can decompose a live spread into an inventory piece and a selection piece by staring at the ladder. The ladder is the state. The decomposition is the story. The lecture keeps them in different sentences on purpose.

Work the earning side once more, on the original touch, for a one-share lift and then for the five-share lift the notes use, so the per-share distance is not confused with a total. Same four-cent hole, opposite signs, and still no informed move, because the lab did not simulate one.

One more state reading, so "state variables" does not stay a phrase. The notes do not state the bid size. You cannot finish a single numeric imbalance, or a single numeric microprice, without a bid size. You can see the imbalance move without freezing the bid size at ten. Let the bid size be q, with q positive, and keep the note's ask of ten. Before the lift, imbalance is q minus ten, over q plus ten. After a five-lot lift the ask size is five, so imbalance is q minus five, over q plus five. Subtract the first ratio from the second over the common denominator, which is q plus five, times q plus ten. The numerator of the difference expands like this. q minus five, times q plus ten, is q squared plus ten q minus five q minus fifty, which is q squared plus five q minus fifty. q minus ten, times q plus five, is q squared plus five q minus ten q minus fifty, which is q squared minus five q minus fifty. Subtract the second expansion from the first. The q squared terms cancel. Five q minus negative five q is ten q. Negative fifty minus negative fifty is zero. The numerator that remains is ten q. The difference of the imbalances is ten q, divided by the product of q plus five and q plus ten. For any positive q that quotient is positive. Eating five lots of the ask raises imbalance. How much it rises still depends on q. The note can say that imbalance moves without ever printing q, and the algebra says the move is upward.
