Change the list by splitting the gateway into a read and a write, which is one structural input, not a new latency number. If the combined mean already added read and write, splitting changes the labels and must not change the total mean. Write that conservation down. People split stages and accidentally invent time. The arithmetic test is whether the sum of the new means equals the old mean. If it does not, you either found a missing wait or you double-counted. Those are the only two honorable explanations. A third explanation, that the split itself costs time, is a new stage, a real one, and it needs its own line rather than a silent bump in both children.

The idea in this block is that means add, and that variances add if the stages are independent. The sum of the means is the mean of the sum. The variance of a sum of independent variables is the sum of the variances. The lab assumes independence and Gaussian stages, so a ninety-ninth percentile is the mean plus two point three three standard deviations. Correlation in the tails breaks that number. The note says so. The slider does not hide it.

Say the mean rule with no probability attached, because it does not need independence. If each stage has a mean, the mean of the sum is the sum of the means, whenever those means exist. Dependence does not touch this sentence. A slow model that tends to happen on the same draws as a slow gateway still contributes its own mean, and the combined mean is still the total of the contributions. If you have five stages with means m1 through m5, the mean of the end-to-end time is m1 plus m2 plus m3 plus m4 plus m5. Drop a stage and you drop its mean. There is no square and no percentile in this paragraph. People add latencies and feel they have done something statistical. So far they have only added means. That part is allowed.

The variance rule is the part that needs a hypothesis. For independent stages, the variance of the sum is the sum of the variances. Standard deviations do not add. The standard deviation of the sum is the square root of the sum of the squared standard deviations. In words, sigma of the sum equals the square root of the sum of sigma i squared. If you add the sigmas themselves, you are computing something larger than this square root, unless all but one of them are zero. The square root of a sum of squares is the length of a vector. The sum of the lengths is the path you walk along the axes. The straight-line length is shorter. That geometry is the entire gap between an honest independent spread and a sum of spreads.

The lab turns the spread into a percentile with a Gaussian assumption. The inverse of the standard normal distribution function, at ninety-nine hundredths, is about two point three two six. The lab uses two point three three. So the lab's ninety-ninth percentile is the mean plus two point three three times the standard deviation of the sum. The difference between two point three three and two point three two six is four thousandths. On one stage, the lab's percentile sits four thousandths of a standard deviation above the more precise normal quantile. That is a rounding choice, and the note states it. It is not the main error available to you. The main error is adding percentiles, which is the next block. A smaller honesty problem sits inside the Gaussian step itself. Latencies are skewed. The note says the Gaussian ninety-ninth percentile is then a lower bound you have not earned. Heavy tails need a measured quantile. The lab's two point three three is for the arithmetic of a bell curve. A trace is for the tail you actually have.

Work two stages with the same standard deviation, call it s, so the square root is visible. Variances s squared and s squared sum to two s squared. The square root of two s squared is s times the square root of two. The square root of two is the positive number whose square is two. One point four one four two squared is one point nine nine nine nine six, which is two for any purpose this rounding can see, short by a few hundred-thousandths. Use one point four one four two. The honest standard deviation of the sum is about one point four one four two s. Multiply by the lab's two point three three. Two point three three times one point four is three point two six two. Two point three three times zero point zero one four is zero point zero three two six two. Two point three three times zero point zero zero zero two is zero point zero zero zero four six six. Add those and you get about three point two nine five s. The honest Gaussian tail piece, above the mean of the sum, is about three point two nine five standard deviations of one stage. The mean of the sum is still just the sum of the means. Nothing in the tail piece changes the means.
