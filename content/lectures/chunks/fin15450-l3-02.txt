Change one coefficient in the equation and recompute the identity, so r is not a decoration. Suppose the financing rate on the premium and on the hedge were 0. The equation would say the time partial plus one half sigma squared S squared times the second partial equals 0, because every term that carried r drops. That is a different problem. Its call price is not the r equal to 0.05 call price. You will compute both in the closed form: with S and K equal to 100, sigma equal to 0.2, and T equal to 1, the zero-rate call is about 7.966, and the five-percent call is about 10.451. The differential equation notices the 0.05. A lab that does not is solving the zero-rate equation by accident. Conversely, leaving mu equal to 0.10 inside the r slots produces the price associated with r equal to 0.10, about 13.270 on those same inputs, which is the wrong financing. The equation’s silence on mu is not vagueness. It is a specific omission, next to a specific presence of r, sigma, S, and the terminal maximum of S minus K and 0.

The idea in this block is the closed form. The call price is S times Phi of d1, minus K times e to the minus r T, times Phi of d2. d2 equals d1 minus sigma times the square root of T. d1 carries the log moneyness, the carry, and half the variance. In words, d1 equals the quantity log of S over K, plus the quantity r plus one half sigma squared, times T, all divided by sigma times the square root of T. Phi is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. The lab uses a standard normal approximation. It is a desk calculator, not a special-function library. Move mu. The call must sit still. Move sigma or r, and it should move.

Build d1 on the base inputs before you touch Phi. Let S equal 100, K equal 100, r equal 0.05, sigma equal 0.2, and T equal 1. The log of S over K is the log of 1, which is 0. One half sigma squared is one half of 0.04, which is 0.02. r plus that half variance is 0.05 plus 0.02, which is 0.07. Times T, which is 1, the numerator is 0 plus 0.07, which is 0.07. The denominator is 0.2 times the square root of 1, which is 0.2. d1 equals 0.07 divided by 0.2, which is 0.35. d2 equals 0.35 minus 0.2, which is 0.15. The gap between them is the total volatility, sigma times the square root of T, and on a one-year option with sigma 0.2 that gap is exactly 0.2. If your d2 is 0.35 minus 0.04, you subtracted the variance instead of the standard deviation over the life, and every later digit is wrong.

Phi of 0.35 is about 0.636831. Phi of 0.15 is about 0.559618. Those are values of the standard normal cumulative, the kind of approximation the lab is allowed to use, and you should treat them as computed values rather than as rounded folklore. The discount factor e to the minus r T is e to the minus 0.05, about 0.951229. The discounted strike is 100 times 0.951229, about 95.1229. The first term of the call is S times Phi of d1, which is 100 times 0.636831, which is 63.6831. The second term is the discounted strike times Phi of d2, about 95.1229 times 0.559618, about 53.2325. The call is 63.6831 minus 53.2325, about 10.451. More digits from the same approximation give about 10.4506. Either way, an at-the-money one-year call, at five percent and twenty percent volatility, is a bit more than ten points on a spot of 100. If your calculator says 20, a factor of two has entered, often from using variance as if it were volatility or from forgetting the discount. If it says 4.88, you may have reported the forward intrinsic value instead of the call. S minus the discounted strike is 100 minus 95.1229, about 4.877. The call, about 10.451, sits above that intrinsic forward value, which is what remaining optionality should do for a call that can still finish out of the money in the risk-neutral picture, even though the forward spot is above the strike.

Mu is the slider that must do nothing. The formula just evaluated has no slot for mu. Put mu equal to 0.1, recompute d1, and you recompute the same 0.35, because the numerator is still log moneyness plus r plus half sigma squared, times T. The call stays about 10.451. Put mu equal to 0.3. Same call. Put mu equal to negative 0.2. Same call. The paths move. The previous lecture says the mean of S at time 1 is 100 times e to the mu, so a mu of 0.1 puts the mean near 110.52 and a mu of 0.3 puts it near 135.0, since e to the 0.3 is about 1.34986. The call does not follow that mean. If the lab’s call follows it, the lab has priced the physical expectation of something, or it has stuffed mu into the r slot, and either way it is not this formula. The exercise is to move the drift across its range, watch the fan, and watch the call sit.

Change sigma, which must move the call. Keep S, K, r, and T as they are, and replace 0.2 by 0.4. One half sigma squared is one half of 0.16, which is 0.08. r plus that is 0.05 plus 0.08, which is 0.13. The numerator is 0.13. The denominator is 0.4. d1 equals 0.325. d2 equals 0.325 minus 0.4, which is negative 0.075. Phi of 0.325 is about 0.627409. Phi of negative 0.075 is about 0.470107. The discount factor did not change, because r and T did not. The first term is 62.7409. The second term is about 95.1229 times 0.470107, about 44.718. The call is about 18.023. Volatility doubled, from 0.2 to 0.4, and the call rose from about 10.451 to about 18.023. It did not double. Nothing in the formula says prices are linear in sigma. d1 moved from 0.35 to 0.325, a small move, while d2 moved from 0.15 to negative 0.075, a larger move, and both Phis moved. The net effect on this at-the-money option is an increase of about 7.57 points. Change sigma the other way, to 0.1. One half sigma squared is 0.005. r plus that is 0.055. Divided by 0.1, d1 equals 0.55. d2 equals 0.55 minus 0.1, which is 0.45. Phi of 0.55 is about 0.708840. Phi of 0.45 is about 0.673645. The first term is about 70.884. The second is about 95.1229 times 0.673645, about 64.079. The call is about 6.805. Lower volatility, lower call, still above the forward intrinsic value 4.877. Three volatilities, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.4, three prices, about 6.805, 10.451, and 18.023. A lab that does not move when sigma moves is frozen. A lab that moves when mu moves is wired to the wrong input.
