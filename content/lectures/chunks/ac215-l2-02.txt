The idea in this block is that reproducibility is a pin, not a vibe. A model card names data, code, and metrics at a commit. Drift is a change in a distribution you specified. If you did not specify the feature distribution, you cannot announce that it drifted.

Read the model card as a set of names that can be pointed at. Data, at a commit, means you can say which data produced the fit. Code, at a commit, means you can say which program produced the fit. Metrics, at a commit, means you can say which numbers were computed, on which data, by which program. A card that says the model works, without those three pins, is a vibe. You cannot regenerate it, and you cannot tell a later change from a later story. The commit is the pin. A description that floats free of a commit can be edited until it matches whatever the model is doing now. That edit is not reproducibility. It is a rewrite.

Drift is narrower than disappointment. Drift is a change in a distribution you specified. The specification comes first. You write down the feature distribution you will compare against, and you write it down before you deploy. A threshold without a reference is a mood. If the share of missing values, or the mean of a feature, or whatever you actually specified, moves past the threshold relative to that reference, you have an announcement you can defend. If you specified nothing, the sentence "it drifted" has no subject. Retrain is an action. It is not the definition of drift. You can retrain because you drifted, or because you changed the objective, or because the card was wrong. Those are different reasons. The action does not define the word.

The hot path does not write the card. Writing the model card is cold. It belongs off the send, on the forbidden-line side of the budget. That does not make it optional for the system. It makes it absent from the latency sum. A design that skips the card in order to save a millisecond has misunderstood which budget the card was on. The card is a pin for the model you might later refuse to put on the hot path. If the net is not on the path, the card still pins the net you run in the cold place, if you run one. If you have no model, you do not invent a card to look complete. The pin is for a model that exists.

Work the order-of-magnitude path next to the card, so a fast number is not mistaken for a pin. Gateway mean g, model mean ten g, pair mean eleven g, model share about ninety-one percent. None of those figures names data, code, or metrics. They can be true of a serving stage whose card is blank. Dominance in the budget is a statement about time. The card is a statement about identity. You need both, and they do not substitute. A beautiful card does not shrink ten g. A tiny g does not identify the model. When you set the model near zero and the pair collapses to the gateway, you still have not pinned a model. You have removed it from the handoff. The card, if the model lives somewhere else, stays with the model. The handoff budget does not inherit the card by being fast.

Change one input in the definition, not in the latency. Suppose you specified the reference mean of one feature, and the deployed mean of that feature moves. You may talk about drift in that feature. Now change the specified object: you never wrote the reference, and you only notice that a trading result got worse. You may not call that worse result drift. You may call it a worse result. The lecture's line is strict because the alternative is a mood. A threshold without a reference is a mood. The changed input is the presence or absence of the reference. Everything else, including how unhappy you are about the result, can stay. The word drift does not survive the deletion of the reference.

You might ask whether a commit hash on the card is enough if the data are described in prose. The lecture says the card names data, code, and metrics at a commit. Prose that does not identify the data does not name the data. A commit of the code, with the data left as "the usual set," does not pin the data. You would not accept a latency budget that said "the usual stages." Do not accept a card that says "the usual data." The standard is the same one you used when you refused to let Kubernetes stand in for a distribution. A name that cannot be resolved is not the object.

What the claim does not mean is that every change in a feature is drift. It is drift only relative to a distribution you specified. It does not mean you must retrain whenever you say the word. Retrain is an action, not the definition. It does not mean the model card belongs in the hot latency sum. Writing it is cold. It does not mean a path with no model needs a card. It means a model you run needs a pin, whether or not that model sits on the nanosecond handoff. It does not mean the order-of-magnitude calculation was a metric on the card. The ten-to-one split is a teaching arithmetic for a budget. The card's metrics are the metrics you name at a commit. Do not paste a lab slider into the card and call it a result. The defaults are invented. Replace them before you defend them, and do not defend them by filing them under reproducibility.
