Name the idea before you touch a model. Dependence is the object. A supervised model that assumes independent rows is a model of a shuffled market. Returns have dependence, volatility clustering, and a clock that is not the row index. You can still train on rows. You cannot score them as if the shuffle were the exchange.

Say the same thing at the speed of a definition. In the usual supervised table, a row is a bundle of inputs and a label. The fitting procedure averages a loss over rows. That average does not consult the order of the rows. The algebra will run if you shuffle. The market will not become the shuffled market. If your score is the score of the shuffled table, you have measured a different experiment and given it the original name.

Dependence here is not a vague warning. It is the claim that the joint behavior of neighboring times is part of the problem. Volatility clustering is one concrete form of that claim. Large moves tend to sit near other large moves. Quiet stretches tend to sit near other quiet stretches. A clock that is not the row index means that row four hundred is a time, or a place in a sequence of times, and not an independent draw that happens to have been written on line four hundred. Adjacent seconds share a regime. That sentence is the whole reason a random split is dangerous.

You are allowed to train on rows. Packing a decision into a feature vector is a representation, not a confession that time is fake. The optimizer can see those vectors one at a time. The prohibition sits on the evaluation. A random split puts the future in the training set. The score is then a memory test. It asks whether the fit remembers a neighborhood it was allowed to peek into, not whether a decision at that second, using only what was knowable then, would have been any good.

Work the split as a count, not as a mood. Take ten consecutive seconds, numbered zero through nine. Suppose the training set is seconds zero, one, three, four, six, and eight, and the test set is seconds two, five, seven, and nine. Four seconds are being scored. Look at each of them and ask whether any later second was placed in training.

Second two is a test second. Seconds three and four are later, and both are in training. That test second is contaminated. Second five is a test second. Seconds six and eight are later and in training. Contaminated. Second seven is a test second. Second eight is later and in training. Contaminated. Second nine is a test second, and nothing in the sample comes after it. That one is clean, and it is clean only because the tape ended. Three of the four test seconds have a future neighbor inside the training set. You do not need a large theorem. You need to compare integers.

Now take the same ten seconds and refuse the shuffle. Train on zero through six. Test on seven, eight, and nine. Every training timestamp is strictly earlier than every test timestamp. The contamination count is zero. This is not yet the full rolling design you will write down later. It is only the opposite of the random partition. The comparison is the lesson. "I held out some rows" is a statement about a set. "I did not train on the future" is a statement about order. They are not the same statement.

Do the count once more on a pattern that looks fair when you glance down a column. Take seconds zero through five. Put the even seconds in training and the odd seconds in test. Training holds zero, two, and four. Test holds one, three, and five. Test second one has two and four in its future, both training. Test second three has four in its future, and four is training. Test second five has no future inside the sample. Two of the three test points are leaked by a split that alternates. Alternation feels mixed. On a clock it is a systematic gift of the future to the fit. The feeling that a spreadsheet looks shuffled is not a check. The timestamps are the check.

If you want the clean contrast on those same six seconds, train on zero, one, and two, and test on three, four, and five. The contamination count is zero. The sample is tiny, so the estimate will be noisy. Noise is not leakage. You may report a noisy honest split. You may not repair the noise by borrowing a later row and then describing the borrow as a larger sample.

A second numerical picture, so clustering is not only a word. This tape is an illustration of neighboring products, not a recording from the lab and not a market. Let the absolute returns across the ten seconds be one, one, one, eight, nine, seven, one, one, two, and one. The large values occupy seconds three, four, and five, counting from zero. The mean of the ten numbers does not care about that clump. Add them: three ones at the start are three, plus eight is eleven, plus nine is twenty, plus seven is twenty-seven, plus one is twenty-eight, plus one is twenty-nine, plus two is thirty-one, plus one is thirty-two. Thirty-two divided by ten is three and one fifth. Any shuffle of the same ten numbers has the same mean.

Neighboring products do care. On the original order the nine successive products are one times one, one times one, one times eight, eight times nine, nine times seven, seven times one, one times one, one times two, and two times one. That list is one, one, eight, seventy-two, sixty-three, seven, one, two, and two. Add it carefully. One plus one is two. Plus eight is ten. Plus seventy-two is eighty-two. Plus sixty-three is one hundred forty-five. Plus seven is one hundred fifty-two. Plus one is one hundred fifty-three. Plus two is one hundred fifty-five. Plus two is one hundred fifty-seven. One hundred fifty-seven over nine is seventeen and four ninths. The size of that average is the cluster: eight times nine, and nine times seven, dominate the sum.

Separate the cluster and the products collapse. Suppose the eight, the nine, and the seven are moved so that none of them touch, for example the order one, eight, one, nine, one, seven, one, two, one, one. The successive products are eight, eight, nine, nine, seven, seven, two, two, and one. Add: eight plus eight is sixteen, plus nine is twenty-five, plus nine is thirty-four, plus seven is forty-one, plus seven is forty-eight, plus two is fifty, plus two is fifty-two, plus one is fifty-three. Fifty-three over nine is five and eight ninths. Same ten numbers, same mean of three and one fifth, and the average neighboring product has fallen from seventeen and four ninths to five and eight ninths. If your evaluation is about what sits next to what, the shuffle is a different question. Volatility clustering is exactly a claim about what sits next to what.

The question I want on the table is whether dependence forbids a model that reads one row at a time. It does not. A row can hold a short trail of earlier information, written into columns before the fit begins. The model can be a function of that row alone. You have either written the dependence into the features or you have chosen not to, but you have not thereby earned the right to shuffle the score. The question is not whether the architecture looks like a table model. The question is whether the number you are about to quote could have been computed at the decision time.
