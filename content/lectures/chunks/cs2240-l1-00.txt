The tape does not rewind. A stream is too long to store. You may keep a sketch whose size you chose, and you may not ask for the previous element again. Market data has this shape even when the file would fit on a disk, because the decision has to happen before the next packet. The contract is one pass, or a pass you can afford, and state that is little-o of the length, ideally polylogarithmic in that length. Failure probability is part of the answer, not a footnote you add after the number looks pretty.

Hear the constraint as a ban on rereading, not as a compliment to compression in general. If you can pause the tape, scan it twice, and keep every symbol, you are not in the setting this lecture is about. You are in ordinary storage. The stream setting starts when a second look is either impossible or too late to be the decision. A market packet that you needed yesterday is not repaired by a file that still holds it. The decision moved on. The sketch is what you had in hand when the packet arrived.

The lab makes the constraint visible by using an alphabet small enough to audit. Eight futures roots fit on the screen. They are small enough that you can know the true count of each one. The sketch is still run as if the alphabet were large. That is deliberate. Comparing an estimate to the truth is the privilege of a toy. If you could not see the true count, a sketch demonstration would be theater. Eight symbols fit, so every overestimate can be seen. The algorithm does not get easier when the alphabet grows. Your ability to audit it does. Remember which half is the privilege.

Do a first numeric pass with the lab’s own opening move. Stream the same symbol five times. The truth for that symbol is five. You have not kept the five events as five records. You have updated a sketch whose size you chose before the five arrived, or at least whose size does not grow with a longer tape. The estimate the sketch returns is at least the truth. Five is the floor for this symbol after this opening. If the estimate is five, some row of the sketch kept a clean look at those arrivals. If the estimate is larger, a collision in some row inflated a counter, and the minimum across rows is what kept the damage from being worse. You will take the mechanism apart in the next lesson. Here you only need the contract: one pass, a bounded sketch, an estimate that is not allowed to pretend it stored the tape, and a truth you can see because the alphabet is eight.

Change one input. Stream that same symbol seven times instead of five, and leave the alphabet at eight. The truth is now seven. The estimate is still at least the truth, so seven is the new floor. You still may not rewind to recount the first five and then add the last two by looking backward. The two extra arrivals are new updates. The sketch either absorbed them or it did not. An estimate of seven meets the floor. An estimate of nine is an overestimate of two, which you are allowed to notice because eight roots fit on the screen, and which you would have no business calling “about right” without a bound. The next beat is where the bound gets its symbols. This beat only insists that seven replaced five because the tape grew, and that the tape still does not rewind.

You might ask why you refuse to store the tape when eight symbols would fit in eight counters. Because the lab is a toy alphabet run as if the alphabet were large. Eight counters would be an exact histogram, and exact histograms are allowed only when the alphabet is tiny. The slide says that directly. The eight roots are tiny, which is why you can audit. The sketch is not granted an easier algorithm in exchange for your audit. If you store eight exact counters, you have left the exercise and solved a different problem, the problem of a tiny alphabet. That solution is honest when you name it as an exact histogram. It is not a sketch, and it will not survive the day the alphabet stops being eight.

What the one-pass contract does not mean is that two passes are immoral, or that a file which happens to fit must be deleted. It means that if your decision cannot wait for a second pass, your state has to be the sketch you chose, and the estimate has to come with a failure probability. It also does not mean that little-o of the length is a specific table you can cite without parameters. The slide’s ideal is polylogarithmic state. A sketch whose size tracks the stream has broken the contract even if the arithmetic of some particular afternoon stayed small. And it does not mean the eight roots are a market. They are a toy small enough for the truth to be visible. A real tape of trades can be dominated by a few symbols, which is why point queries matter more than a full histogram, and that domination is not a promise that eight will be the number.

Count what “too long to store” is refusing, with the five-arrival opening and the eight-symbol alphabet side by side. Five arrivals of one symbol, if stored raw, are five records. A mix that follows, say the lab’s mix after the five, adds more records. You do not get a budget for those records. You get a sketch of a size you chose. The exact alternative, eight counters, is constant in the length of the tape and linear in the alphabet. Constant in the length satisfies a memory bound. Linear in an alphabet of eight is cheap only because eight is tiny. The lecture’s warning is that you must not learn the wrong lesson from the cheapness. When the alphabet grows, eight counters become a dictionary of the alphabet, which is the storage the stream setting refused. The sketch’s job is to stay sized by the guarantee you ask for, not by the number of distinct roots that showed up.

Suppose you cheat once and rewind after the seven arrivals, recount, and fix the estimate down to the truth. You have produced a pretty number. You have also left the definition. An algorithm that returns a pretty number is not, by that prettiness, a streaming algorithm. The next beat will say what replaces prettiness: a named norm, an epsilon, a delta. Until then, notice that the rewind made the seven exact and taught you nothing about a packet that cannot be reread. Market data that must be decided before the next packet does not offer you that recount. The file fitting on disk does not offer it either, if the decision has already gone by.

A second small change of input, on the alphabet rather than the repetition count. Keep five identical arrivals, and imagine you audited only four of the eight roots because the screen was crowded. You can still see those four truths. You cannot claim the sketch behaved on the roots you did not look at. The privilege of comparing estimate to truth is limited to the symbols you actually counted by hand. The algorithm’s obligation is not limited that way. It runs as if the alphabet were large, including the roots you skipped. Partial audit is still a toy. It is a smaller toy. Do not promote it to a proof that collisions were absent in the half you ignored.
