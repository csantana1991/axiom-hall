The idea in this block is that you do not add percentiles. The ninety-ninth percentile of A plus the ninety-ninth percentile of B is not the ninety-ninth percentile of A plus B. It is a pessimistic cartoon that becomes a service-level lie if you ship it as a percentile. If you need the tail of the sum, add the variances under a hypothesis you will defend, or measure the sum. The lab prints both the illegal sum of the per-stage percentiles and the independent Gaussian percentile, so you can see the gap.

Write the two expressions one above the other, in words. For a single Gaussian stage, the lab's percentile is the stage mean plus two point three three times the stage standard deviation. The illegal combination adds those percentiles. Its value is the sum of the means, plus two point three three times the sum of the standard deviations. The honest independent combination is the sum of the means, plus two point three three times the square root of the sum of the squared standard deviations. The sums of the means match. Every bit of the gap is in the tail piece. The gap equals two point three three times the quantity you get when you subtract the square root of the sum of squares from the sum of the standard deviations. That quantity is positive whenever at least two standard deviations are positive. It is zero when all but one are zero, which is the edge case that lets the bad habit look correct.
