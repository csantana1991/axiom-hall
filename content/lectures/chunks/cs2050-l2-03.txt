After that layout is true, a wide register can still be the wrong tool on the sequence number and a possible tool on a cold feature path. None of the last-mile choices revise the model charges. If you want a single numeric check you can do on the board before you leave, do the pin-and-pad combination the wrong way and then the right way. Wrong way: head at offset zero, tail at offset eight, both threads pinned to two cores. The pin is present and the line is shared, so the model still charges eight, and the pin has not bought the missing stride. Right way: head at a multiple of sixty-four, tail sixty-four bytes later, threads unpinned. The model charges one, and you accept migration only if the measurement says the warmth loss matters. Then, and only then, you consider the operating-system setting. The order is the lecture. Layout first. Measurement next. Pinning last, and not in the header.

A dependent index stays scalar in both the wrong way and the right way. Suppose the head ticket is nineteen and the capacity is a power of two so the mask is the right address calculation, a fact the mask lab is for and this beat does not redo. The slot you compute depends on that ticket. A wide register does not compute sixteen next slots unless the protocol actually has sixteen independent tickets, which a single producer head does not. One ticket, one slot, one consumer matching it. Width does not multiply the protocol. That is what “does not vectorize into a metaphor” is protecting. You may use the width where the slide allows, on the cold feature path, after the handoff has already moved a record you are free to reread in bulk.
