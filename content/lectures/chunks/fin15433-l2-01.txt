Work the earning side once more, on the original touch, for a one-share lift and then for the five-share lift the notes use, so the per-share distance is not confused with a total. One share lifting the ask pays one hundred point zero two. Against the decision mid of one hundred, the distance is two cents. Five shares pay the same price per share if they do not walk, which they do not when the ask has ten lots and you take five. Five times two cents is ten cents. The ten cents is five copies of the same two-cent distance. It is not a deeper spread. The spread is still four cents, and the half-spread is still two. If you later call the ten cents a shortfall, you are using the buyer's sign, not the maker's. The maker who sold those five collected two cents a share relative to the mid, before fees, on the uninformed branch. The buyer who decided at the mid paid two cents a share, before fees. Same four-cent hole, opposite signs, and still no informed move, because the lab did not simulate one.

One more state reading, so "state variables" does not stay a phrase. On the original touch you can name the best bid, the best ask, the spread, and the mid, and you have done so: ninety-nine point nine eight, one hundred point zero two, four cents, and one hundred. You cannot yet name a size. The notes put a ten-lot ask into the next calculation, and they never state the bid size in this paragraph. A spread story that needs imbalance is not finished by the prices alone. Prices fix the hole. Sizes fix the microprice and the imbalance, which are the other state variables the lecture will make you recompute after you yourself change the book. Until a size is written down, do not invent one and then call it part of Glosten and Milgrom's paragraph. This paragraph used prices. The sizes come when your own order moves the state.

The idea in this block is that your own order moves the state. Lifting five lots reduces ask size or walks the ask. The microprice updates because you changed the book, not because the world revealed news. Impact starts at one lot. Calling a one-lot change negligible is a measurement you have not done.

Start from the touch you already computed, and add the one size the notes give. The ask is ten lots at one hundred point zero two. The bid remains ninety-nine point nine eight. You lift five. Five is less than ten, so you do not walk. You pay one hundred point zero two for all five. The best ask stays one hundred point zero two, because the level still has size. The remaining ask size is ten minus five, which is five. The best bid does not change. The spread stays four cents. The mid stays one hundred. If your only pictures of the state are spread and mid, the lift looks like a nonevent. That is the mistake the block is built to stop.

The notes do not state the bid size. You cannot finish a single numeric imbalance, or a single numeric microprice, without a bid size. The case you can finish without inventing a second, unrelated size is the case that sets the bid size equal to the ask size you were given. Set the bid size to ten before the lift. State that as a supposition, not as a fact the note printed. Before the lift, the two sizes match. The microprice formula weights the ask by the bid size and the bid by the ask size, then divides by the sum of the sizes. Ten times one hundred point zero two is one thousand point two zero. Ten times ninety-nine point nine eight is nine hundred ninety-nine point eight zero. The sum is two thousand exactly. The denominator is twenty. Two thousand divided by twenty is one hundred. The microprice equals the mid, which is what the formula does whenever the sizes match.

Imbalance before the lift, under the same supposition, is ten minus ten, divided by ten plus ten. The numerator is zero. The ratio is zero. After you lift five, the ask size is five and the bid size is still ten. Imbalance is ten minus five, divided by ten plus five, which is five fifteenths, which is one third. The ratio moved from zero to one third because you ate ask size. The microprice after the lift is ten times one hundred point zero two, plus five times ninety-nine point nine eight, all over fifteen. Ten times one hundred point zero two is one thousand point two zero. Five times ninety-nine point nine eight is four hundred ninety-nine point nine zero. The sum is one thousand five hundred point one zero. Divide by fifteen. Fifteen times one hundred is one thousand five hundred, and the remaining ten hundredths divided by fifteen is one hundred fiftieth, which is zero point zero zero six repeating. The microprice is one hundred point zero zero six repeating. It sits two thirds of a cent above the mid. Nothing in the outside world announced a new value. You removed five lots from the ask, the sizes stopped matching, and both state variables moved.

You can see the imbalance move without freezing the bid size at ten. Let the bid size be q, with q positive, and keep the note's ask of ten. Before the lift, imbalance is q minus ten, over q plus ten. After a five-lot lift the ask size is five, so imbalance is q minus five, over q plus five. Subtract the first ratio from the second over the common denominator, which is q plus five, times q plus ten. The numerator of the difference expands like this. q minus five, times q plus ten, is q squared plus ten q minus five q minus fifty, which is q squared plus five q minus fifty. q minus ten, times q plus five, is q squared plus five q minus ten q minus fifty, which is q squared minus five q minus fifty. Subtract the second expansion from the first. The q squared terms cancel. Five q minus negative five q is ten q. Negative fifty minus negative fifty is zero. The numerator that remains is ten q. The difference of the imbalances is ten q, divided by the product of q plus five and q plus ten. For any positive q that quotient is positive. Eating five lots of the ask raises imbalance. How much it rises still depends on q. The note can say that imbalance moves without ever printing q, and the algebra says the move is upward. It does not hand you a single decimal until you write q down, which is why the lab brief tells you to write imbalance down before you click.
