A heavy-hitter story that stays inside the lecture is the slide’s own. A few symbols dominate a tape of trades. Point queries matter more than a full histogram. That is a reason to estimate frequencies of named symbols rather than to materialize every bin. It is not a reason to report the zeroth moment from a structure that never tracked distinctness, and it is not a reason to skip epsilon and delta. Domination explains why you care about a point. The guarantee from the previous beat explains what you are allowed to claim about the estimate. The one-pass contract explains why you do not store the tape in order to answer the point. Three beats, three different refusals.

Try a distinct-count brag in numbers and watch it fail honestly. Alphabet of eight. You query all eight roots in a count-min sketch after the tape of frequencies seven, one, one, and one, with four roots absent. Suppose collisions make two absent roots return an estimate of one, while the true absent count is zero. A naive “how many estimates were positive” returns six. The zeroth moment is four. Six is not four. The sketch did not malfunction as a point estimator merely because an absent root picked up a collision. Overestimation of a point is the behavior you will prove one-sided in the next lesson. Using those overestimates as a distinct count is the brag the lecture forbids. If both absent roots had stayed at estimate zero and the four present roots stayed positive, you might have printed four by luck. Luck on eight roots is the toy’s privilege. It is not a distinct-count algorithm.

Change the heavy frequency once more, from seven to seven on two different roots, so the second moment’s punishment is shared. Frequencies seven and seven. Zeroth moment two. First moment fourteen. Second moment forty-nine plus forty-nine, which is ninety-eight. Each heavy root is half the second moment, and a point query is still a query about one of them, aiming at seven, not at ninety-eight and not at two. A count-min sketch, asked about one root, is in its job. Asked “how many distinct,” it is out of its job. Asked for the second moment as a single scalar, it is out of its job as well. You said the moments once. You do not blend them back together in the last minute of the hour.

The lab’s five identical arrivals are the smallest case of the same split. Zeroth moment one, if nothing else arrived. First moment five. Second moment twenty-five. Point frequency five. The estimate is at least five. If it prints five, you matched the point frequency on this toy. You have not measured a distinct-count guarantee, because there was only one symbol to count and the alphabet was eight. Stream the mix the lab asks for after the five, recompute the three moments by hand, and only then look at the sketch. The sketch owes you a point estimate at least as large as the truth for the symbol you query. It does not owe you the zeroth moment, and it does not owe you the square sum. Those are different questions, said once, on purpose, so the later hour can be about one structure without pretending it is all of them.
