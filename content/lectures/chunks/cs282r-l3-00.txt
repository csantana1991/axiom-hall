Name the idea in the blunt form you can use under pressure. A future mid in the feature is not a feature. Label construction is where leakage hides. If the label is the next return and the feature uses a window centered on now, half of the window is the label's accomplice. Centered smoothers are guilty until proven causal. Any input not knowable at decision time counts, and that includes a scaler fit on the full sample and a vocabulary built with the test set.

The label is allowed to see the future. That is the point of a label. The next return is a function of a later price. You will compute it when you build the training table, and you will compute it when you score a forecast after the fact. The line you may not cross is the line between that label and the inputs that were supposed to be known when the decision was made. Leakage is what you get when the later price takes a side door into those inputs. The side door is often a smoother with a pleasant name, a normalization you applied once to the whole file, or a dictionary of categories you built before you cut the sample.

Work a centered window on prices that make the accomplice visible. This path is an illustration, not the lab. The mids at times zero through four are ten, ten, ten, twelve, and twelve. Put now at time two, so the current mid is ten. The next mid, at time three, is twelve. The next simple return is twelve divided by ten, minus one, which is one fifth. A window of width five centered on now uses times zero, one, two, three, and four. Two of those five times are strictly after now. The lecture's short sentence calls half the window the accomplice. On this count the strictly future portion is two fifths, and one of those two future mids is exactly the mid inside the label. The average of the five mids is ten plus ten plus ten plus twelve plus twelve, which is fifty-four, divided by five, which is ten and four fifths. The current mid is ten. The smoothed feature sits four fifths of a point above the current mid, and both of those four fifths came from the two future twelves. Ten and four fifths minus ten is four fifths, and four fifths divided by two future steps is two fifths of a point of future mid in each of those steps, which matches the jump from ten to twelve. You did not need a subtle statistic. The feature moved because the label's price moved.

A causal window on the same clock does not move. Take times zero, one, and two only, the three mids knowable at time two if the mid at two is known at the decision. Their average is ten. Subtract the current mid and you get zero. The jump to twelve is invisible, which is what you want in a feature, because that jump is the label. Prove the smoother was causal, or do not use it. A picture that looks smooth because it averaged tomorrow into today is not a denoised version of today. It is tomorrow, diluted.

Change the label's timing and watch the same window commit a slightly different theft. Suppose the label is not the one-step return but the two-step return, from the mid at time two to the mid at time four. Time four is twelve, time two is ten, and the simple return is again one fifth. The centered window still contains time three and time four. Time four is the end of the label. Time three is the path in between. Both are unknowable at time two. If your defense is that you did not use the next mid, only a nearby mid, time three is the next mid and time four is the one after. You used both. Centered smoothers are guilty until proven causal means you do not get to start from the assumption that a symmetric filter is harmless and then wait for someone to prosecute it. You start from the assumption that it reads the future, and you replace it with a window whose last timestamp is the decision time.

The scaler is the same crime in a different uniform. Suppose the raw feature on three training days is zero, two, and four, and on two test days it is ten and twelve. This is a constructed file, so you can see the mean move. The training-only mean is zero plus two plus four, divided by three, which is two. A training-only centering subtracts two. The training values become negative two, zero, and two. The test values, centered with the same training mean, become eight and ten. You have not used the test days to define the transformation. Now fit the center on the full sample, which is the mistake. The five numbers sum to twenty-eight. The mean is twenty-eight fifths, or five and three fifths. Subtract that from the training value four and you get four minus five and three fifths, which is negative one and three fifths. The same recorded feature, on a training day, has changed from plus two under an honest center to negative one and three fifths under a leaky center. The test values ten and twelve pulled the origin up by three and three fifths, and every training row slid with it. The model is allowed to see a version of the past that already knows the future average. A scaler fit on the full sample is leakage even when the formula for the scaler is printed in a textbook and feels like preprocessing rather than like a feature.

Do the vocabulary version with the same strictness, because it does not look like a price. Suppose the training days only ever produce two imbalance bins, call them low and high, and a test day produces a third bin, call it extreme. A vocabulary built on the training days has two entries. The test day is out of that vocabulary, and you need a rule you froze in advance for unseen bins, such as a single unknown bucket that was already available before the test day existed. A vocabulary built with the test set has three entries. The extreme bin receives its own coordinate because you looked at the test day. That coordinate is an input that was not knowable as a named direction in the feature space at training time, and it is not knowable at a decision time that precedes the test day. Includes a vocabulary built with the test set means exactly this. The dictionary is part of the model. If the dictionary was compiled with the future, the model was compiled with the future.

A lag is the same test with a friendlier reputation, so count it on the same mids. The mids were ten, ten, ten, twelve, and twelve at times zero through four, and now is time two. A feature defined as the mid one step ahead is twelve, the mid at time three. That twelve is not a lag. A lag looks backward. The mid at time one, which is ten, is a lag of one step and is knowable at time two if you froze that rule. The mid at time three is a lead, and a lead of the thing inside the label is the label's accomplice with the sign reversed. People say lag when they mean "a shift," and a shift of the wrong sign is leakage. Write the sign. If the code says shift by minus one and your convention is that negative looks forward, you have built a lead and named it a lag. The timestamp test does not care what the function was called. It cares that time three is after time two. Ten is legal. Twelve is not. The centered average of ten and four fifths was illegal because it mixed the legal tens with that twelve and the twelve after it. Removing the name "smooth" and writing "lead" does not repair it, and writing "lag" on a negative shift does not repair it either.
