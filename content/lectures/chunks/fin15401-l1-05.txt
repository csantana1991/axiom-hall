Add a target return and the slice is no longer a point. You still require the weights to sum to 1, and you now require the weighted mean to equal a number. Two equalities, three weights, one free direction left. Many portfolios hit the same target mean. The quadratic program, or the two-by-two system you get from the derivatives when the matrix is diagonal, picks the one of those portfolios with the smallest variance. A long-only book then spends that leftover freedom on inequalities, a weight at least zero, a weight at most a cap, until the free direction is gone or pinned to a boundary. None of that extra structure appeared in the two-asset target arithmetic, where the weight two thirds was forced before variance was allowed an opinion. The contrast is the point of the slide. Two assets, two equalities, pinned. More assets, a subspace, then constraints.

You might ask whether minimizing variance instead of half variance changes the portfolio you deliver. It does not. The one half cancels the 2 from the derivative, and the constraint set does not mention the factor. The minimizer of variance and the minimizer of half variance, on the same equalities, are the same weight vector. You can check it on the target of 0.10. The weight two thirds was pinned by the means before any objective was evaluated. The objective only scored it, and the score was a variance of about 0.02917 whether or not you halve it afterward. Halving would change the number you hand to a solver as the optimal value. It would not change the holdings.

Here is what the differentiable objective does not mean. It does not mean every portfolio problem in two assets needs an iterative solver. You just pinned five targets with a linear equation and scored them with the quadratic. It does not mean a long-only book with extra constraints remains a two-line solve. Once inequalities bind, more than one weight can sit on a boundary, and the cosmetic one half does not tell you which boundaries. That is a quadratic program. It does not mean a target return is the same problem as global minimum variance. Global minimum variance does not mention mu sub p. The mean falls out after you minimize. In the example it fell out near 0.1018. Targeting 0.14 forced you onto the noisier asset and a variance of 0.0625. Those are different points, and only one of them is the marked minimum the lab is drawing. It does not mean a covariance that fails to be positive semidefinite becomes acceptable because the objective looks smooth. The illegal matrix from the first board, determinant negative 0.000396, can still be written into the formula one half w transpose capital sigma w. The formula will not stop you. The picture is still nonsense, because some direction has negative curvature and the critical point is not a minimum. Differentiability is not a substitute for the semidefinite requirement. You minimize a quadratic that deserves the name variance, subject to the equalities you actually imposed, and you call the result by the name of that problem, not by the name of a neighboring problem you wish you had solved.
