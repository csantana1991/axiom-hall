What the claim does not mean is the boundary of a hedging price. It does not mean the call is the expected payoff under the physical mu, discounted or not. Under mu equal to 0.1 the mean of S is about 110.5, and a naive discount of a forward intrinsic computed off that mean is not 10.451 and is not this derivation. It does not mean a deep call approaches S minus K. It approaches S minus the discounted strike, about 34.877 rather than 30 when S is 130 and K is 100 at five percent and one year, with the formula a little above that at about 35.440 when sigma is 0.2. It does not mean parity is a second independent price you can fit until it matches. Parity, with no dividend, is C minus P equals S minus K e to the minus r T. The lab prices the call. If you later price a put, the difference is the test. It does not mean the normal approximation is the model. The model is the hedging equation. Phi is how the solution is written. A bad approximation of Phi is a numerical error. A dependence on mu is a different model, smuggled in. It does not mean the fan is a picture of the hedge. The fan is the physical skeleton. The call is the hedging price. You should be able to move mu from 0.1 to 0.3, move the mean of S from about 110.5 to about 135, leave d1 at 0.35 on the base inputs, and leave the call at about 10.451. That stillness is the lecture.


Take a longer contract and do every slot again, because a one-year at-the-money number is easy to memorize and then misapply. Let S and K stay at 100, r stay at 0.05, sigma stay at 0.2, and change only T, from 1 to 2. The log moneyness is still 0. The half variance is still 0.02 per year. r plus half variance is still 0.07 per year. Over two years the numerator is 0.14. The denominator is sigma times the square root of 2. The square root of 2 is about 1.41421, times 0.2 is about 0.28284. d1 equals 0.14 divided by 0.28284, about 0.49497. d2 equals d1 minus 0.28284, about 0.21213. You can also see d2 as 0.49497 minus the total volatility. Phi of about 0.49497 is about 0.689691. Phi of about 0.21213 is about 0.583998. The discount factor is e to the minus 0.10, about 0.904837. The discounted strike is about 90.4837. The first term is 100 times 0.689691, about 68.969. The second term is 90.4837 times 0.583998, about 52.842. The call is about 16.127. The forward intrinsic value is 100 minus 90.4837, about 9.516. The call exceeds it by about 6.61. Compare with the one-year call of about 10.451 on the same spot, strike, rate, and volatility. Two years did not double ten and a half into twenty-one. The price went to about 16.13. Total volatility went from 0.2 to about 0.283, a factor of the square root of 2, and the discount on the strike deepened from about 0.951 to about 0.905. Both effects are in the formula. Neither is a doubling.

Mu still has no seat on the two-year contract. The numerator is 0.14 because r T is 0.10 and half sigma squared T is 0.04. A physical drift of 0.10 would have contributed mu T equal to 0.20 if someone forced it into the r slot, and the numerator would have been 0.20 plus 0.04, which is 0.24, and d1 would have been 0.24 over 0.28284, about 0.848, a different number and a different price. Leaving mu at 0.10 in the path generator and out of d1 leaves d1 at about 0.495 and the call at about 16.127. The two-year mean of S, from the previous lecture, would be 100 times e to the mu times 2. At mu equal to 0.10 that is 100 times e to the 0.20, about 122.14. The call does not become a discount of 122 minus something. It stays 16.127. The longer horizon makes the temptation worse, because the physical mean and the spot diverge more, and the formula still ignores the divergence.

Now an out-of-the-money spot, so Phi is not stuck near one half and the price is not a two-digit habit. Let S equal 70, K equal 100, r equal 0.05, sigma equal 0.2, and T equal 1. The log of 70 over 100 is the log of 0.7, about negative 0.356675. The carry piece is still 0.07. The numerator is about negative 0.286675. Divided by 0.2, d1 is about negative 1.4334. d2 is about negative 1.6334. Phi of negative 1.4334 is about 0.07588. Phi of negative 1.6334 is about 0.05120. Both tails are thin. The first term is 70 times 0.07588, about 5.311. The discounted strike is still about 95.1229, times 0.05120 is about 4.870. The call is about 0.441. The forward intrinsic value is 70 minus 95.123, about negative 25.123, and the call does not follow it below zero. A call is at least zero because the terminal maximum of S minus K and 0 is at least zero, and the closed form respects that. Parity then puts the put at the call minus the forward intrinsic value, which is 0.441 minus negative 25.123, about 25.564. The put is the valuable contract when the spot is 70 and the strike is 100. The lab does not display it. Parity does, once you trust the call. If a later put solver says 10 on these inputs, it has failed the test by about 15 points, and you do not need a new theory to see the failure. The difference should be S minus the discounted strike, and it is not.

Change only the rate by one percentage point, from 0.05 to 0.06, on the original at-the-money one-year contract, so a small financing edit has a small, fully visible price edit. Sigma is 0.2, S and K are 100, T is 1. One half sigma squared is 0.02. r plus that is 0.08. d1 equals 0.08 over 0.2, which is 0.4. d2 equals 0.2. Phi of 0.4 is about 0.655422. Phi of 0.2 is about 0.579260. The discount factor is e to the minus 0.06, about 0.941765. The first term is about 65.542. The second term is about 94.1765 times 0.579260, about 54.553. The call is about 10.990. The move from r equal to 0.05 to r equal to 0.06 raised the call from about 10.451 to about 10.990, a difference of about 0.54 points for one percentage point of rate, on this contract. That difference is not a constant you may carry to the two-year option or to the spot of 70. It is one subtraction of two prices. The equation said r would enter. The closed form shows the size, here, of about half a point. Mu moving by the same one percentage point, from wherever it sat in the path generator, moves the call by zero.

One check remains on the two-year d2, because the subtraction is where total volatility is lost. d1 was about 0.49497 and the square root of T times sigma was about 0.28284. Subtract: 0.49497 minus 0.28284 equals 0.21213, which matches the d2 that went into Phi. If the subtraction is skipped, Phi of d1 gets used twice, and the call becomes 68.969 minus 90.4837 times 0.689691. That second product is about 62.406, and the fake call is about 6.563, far from 16.127, and far from the forward intrinsic value in the wrong direction relative to a careless story. Using one Phi for both terms is not a conservative approximation. It is a different formula. d2 is d1 minus sigma times the square root of T every time, including when that gap is 0.2 on the one-year base case and when it is about 0.283 on the two-year case.
