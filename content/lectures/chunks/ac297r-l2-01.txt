Second wrong publish: the producer stores old head plus two. The first push publishes two on slot zero, and the head counter is one. The consumer wants one. It sees two. The difference against tail plus one is positive rather than zero. In the general loop a positive difference means you are not the owner of this observation, so you reload and try again. You can spin there, watching a ticket that jumped over the value you needed. The lab's ticket on that cell will not match your paper, and the instruction is to believe the lab. The legal sequence of tickets on slot zero in the successful walk was zero, then one, then four, then five. Anything that skips one, or that returns to zero, is the off-by-one you were sent to find. After you find it, read the C plus plus and match the release store to the step that published one, not to the step that incremented the head counter. Those are different stores. The head counter becomes one when you claim. The cell becomes one when you release. Same integer, different location, and the consumer is watching the cell.

The question that belongs to this walk is why the consumer waits for tail plus one, rather than for the sequence to equal the tail, or for the sequence to equal the head. The producer publishes head plus one, not head. After the first push, the head counter has already moved to one, and the cell holds one, so a casual glance says everything is one. The consumer's tail is still zero. If the consumer waited for the sequence to equal the tail, it would wait for zero, and the cell no longer holds zero. It holds the publish, which is the old head plus one, and the old head was the tail at the time of the empty ring, so the publish equals tail plus one. Use a different comparison and you will pop an empty ring or skip a full one. On the first pop, tail plus one is one, and the sequence is one. That match is the permission. After the pop, the consumer does not publish tail plus one. It publishes tail plus capacity, which is a ticket for a later lap, not a second copy of the same permission.

What the claim does not mean is that the monotonic counters wrap when the slot wraps, or that a published ticket of five means five live events. After this push, the head is five and the tail is one. The number of events sitting in the ring is four: the three that were never popped, plus the new one. Five minus one is four, which happens to be the capacity, and the ring is full again. You do not need that subtraction stored in a shared size field. You need the tickets. The claim also does not mean the fifth push was a partial write. The difference was negative, so the producer returned before the write. The payload from the first event stayed in the cell until the pop read it, and only then did a later payload replace it, after the ticket had moved to four and the producer had matched it. Order is the protocol. A write that races the read is a broken listing, not a variant.

The idea that makes the tickets worth the trouble is full and empty. Both can show the same masked index. Empty means the consumer's ticket has not been published. Full means the producer's ticket has not come back around. A difference less than zero is the test. A separate size field would be another shared counter. You do not need it.

Go back to the start of the capacity-four ring, before any push, and look at the indexes with a cold eye. Head is zero, tail is zero, and both mask to slot zero. The same index is showing. Acquire slot zero's sequence, which is zero. For the producer, the sequence equals the head, the difference is zero, and the ring is not full. For the consumer, the sequence would need to equal tail plus one, which is one, and the sequence is zero. Zero minus one is negative. The difference is less than zero, so the consumer's ticket has not been published. The ring is empty. Same slot, opposite permissions. If you had inferred the state from the masked index alone, you would have called one situation by one name, and you would have been wrong about one of the two roles.

Now stand at the moment after four successful pushes and before any pop. Head is four, tail is zero. Four masks to slot zero. Zero masks to slot zero. The same index is showing again. Slot zero's sequence is one. For the producer, one minus four is negative, so the producer's ticket has not come back around. The ring is full. The fifth push returns false. For the consumer, tail plus one is one, and the sequence is one. The consumer may read. The ring is not empty. Again one index, and again the two roles disagree. Full and empty are not two names for the same bitmask. They are two failures of two different comparisons, and in this picture only one of the failures is present.

Finish the lap so you can see the empty case return at a nonzero counter. Continue from the tickets you had after the extra push, which were five, two, three, and four, with head five and tail one. Pop the remaining events one by one, and publish tail plus capacity each time.

The tail is one, the slot is one, the sequence is two, and tail plus one is two. Match. Claim the tail, now two. Publish one plus four, which is five. Tickets: five, five, three, four.

The tail is two, the slot is two, the sequence is three, tail plus one is three. Match. Claim the tail, now three. Publish two plus four, which is six. Tickets: five, five, six, four.

The tail is three, the slot is three, the sequence is four, tail plus one is four. Match. Claim the tail, now four. Publish three plus four, which is seven. Tickets: five, five, six, seven.

The tail is four, the slot is zero, the sequence is five, tail plus one is five. Match. This is the event you pushed on the second lap. Claim the tail, now five. Publish four plus four, which is eight. Tickets: eight, five, six, seven. Head is five. Tail is five.

Try one more pop. Tail five masks to slot one. The sequence there is five. Tail plus one is six. Five minus six is negative. The consumer's ticket has not been published. Empty. Stop. You popped five events: the original four, and the one extra push. The notes tell you to pop until empty. This is empty.

Look at the indexes at the end. Head five and tail five both mask to slot one. Same index, and the ring is empty, which you know from the negative consumer difference, not from the mask. The producer, if you asked, would see sequence five against head five, difference zero, and would be allowed to write slot one. Empty for the consumer and writable for the producer, at the same masked index. That is the dual of the full picture, where the producer was refused and the consumer was allowed, also at slot zero.

A size field would seem to make this easier, and it would be a mistake. Suppose you kept a shared counter of how many events are inside, incremented on push and decremented on pop. That counter is touched by both threads on every operation. You have created exactly the kind of shared hot location the tickets were designed to avoid, and you have to define its atomic update, its order relative to the payload, and its behavior when push fails. The protocol you are walking does not need it. The producer learns fullness because the cell sequence is still behind the head. The consumer learns emptiness because the cell sequence is still behind tail plus one. The information is on the cell, published with the release that already had to exist for the payload. A second counter would be another protocol, not a convenience.
