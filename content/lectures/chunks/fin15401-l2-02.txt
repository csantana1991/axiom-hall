Here is what one fund does not mean. It does not mean every investor holds the market, and it does not mean the tangency portfolio is unique in the world. It is unique for a shared mu, a shared capital sigma, and a shared r sub f, up to the normalization, and only when the matrix is well enough behaved to invert. It does not mean leverage is a detail you can ignore when you report holdings. The person with y about 0.617 and the person with y about 1.234 hold the same risky weights and very different balances of cash and borrowing. Reporting only 27 over 79 and 52 over 79 hides the scale separation was invented to isolate. It does not mean a grid is a sloppy substitute for the inverse. On two assets the grid is a legitimate evaluation of the Sharpe ratio, and here it agreed. It does not survive a constraint. The board in front of you is titled do not divide by a tiny sigma. Sharpe ratios explode when a mix has almost no variance. Near rho equal to negative 1 the scanner can crown a numerical ghost. The lab refuses non-finite slopes and shows the weights it actually scored. Your job on this board is to recognize the ghost before a large slope becomes a story.

Keep a pair that can actually cancel. Standard deviations 0.10 and 0.30, means 0.06 and 0.12, risk-free rate 0.03. You already know that at rho equal to negative 1 the variance hits zero at weight 0.75 on the first asset, and the mean there is 0.75 times 0.06 plus 0.25 times 0.12, which is 0.075. The excess over 0.03 is 0.045. A zero denominator and a positive excess is not a large Sharpe ratio. It is not a number. The lab's refusal of non-finite slopes is this case. If you let a scanner print infinity, or print a huge stand-in, you have crowned the ghost.

Watch the explosion approach, with the weights sitting still, so you cannot pretend the portfolio is wandering into a clever new hedge. At rho equal to negative 0.50 the covariance is negative 0.015. At rho equal to negative 0.90 the same normalized weights, 0.75 and 0.25, still solve the proportionality, the standard deviation falls to about 0.0335, and the Sharpe ratio rises to about 1.34. The lab's other duty is to show the weights it actually scored. A slope of 13 without the weights is a rumor. A scanner that reports only the winning slope has hidden the only diagnostic you have. Refusing the non-finite value is half the behavior. Printing the weights is the other half.
