After the two augmentations, the reachable set is S, A, and B. T is not in it. The cut is the pair of sets, source side S, A, B, and sink side T. The capacity of a cut is the sum of the original capacities of edges that leave the source side and enter the sink side. The edges that do that are A to T, capacity 8, and B to T, capacity 10. The sum is 18. There is no edge from S to T. A to B does not cross, because B is on the source side. The flow you pushed has value 18. The cut capacity is 18. They match. Write the theorem in words. The maximum of v of f equals the minimum of c of S, T. Here one flow attains 18, and one cut has capacity 18, so both are optimal. No feasible flow can be larger than 18, because every unit of value must cross every cut, and this cut has only 18 to give. No cut can be smaller than 18, because this flow already pushes 18, and a cut cannot be smaller than a feasible value.

Price the other source sides so 18 is not an uncompared accident. The source side containing only S has leaving edges S to A and S to B, capacities 10 and 10, total 20. The source side S and A has leaving edges S to B, A to T, and A to B, capacities 10, 8, and 4, total 22. The source side S and B has leaving edges S to A and B to T, capacities 10 and 10, total 20. A to B points into this source side, so it is not part of the cut capacity. The source side S, A, and B totals 18, and it is the smallest of the four. On this graph the minimum cut is unique. The reachable-set rule found it without you having to list the other three, but listing them is how you see that the rule and the definition agree.

A to B is a decoy, and the notes mean that precisely. A maximum flow need not use every edge. In the Edmonds–Karp assignment, f of S A is 8, f of S B is 10, f of A T is 8, f of B T is 10, and f of A B is 0. The decoy stayed idle. The cut into T is saturated anyway. A to T is full. B to T is full. If your algorithm saturates the cut, it is done whether or not the decoy moved. Idleness on A to B is not a bug in the search. It is a reminder that edges inside a side, or edges that are not needed to push the optimum, do not have to carry flow.

The flow of value 18 is not unique, and you should build a second one so you do not confuse the certificate with a particular path packing. Ignore the shortest-path rule for a moment and push along S, A, B, T by 4. Then f of S A is 4, f of A B is 4, and f of B T is 4. Value 4. Next push S, A, T by the remaining room, which is the minimum of 6 on S to A and 8 on A to T, so push 6. Now f of S A is 10 and f of A T is 6. Value 10. A is balanced: 10 in, 6 out to T and 4 out to B. Next push S, B, T by the remaining room into T on that edge. B to T has capacity 10 and already carries 4, so the residual is 6, and S to B still has 10. Push 6. Now f of S B is 6 and f of B T is 10. Value 16. B is balanced: 6 from S plus 4 from A equals 10 out to T. T receives 6 plus 10, which is 16. One more edit remains. Residual S to B is 4. Residual B to A is 4, because A to B carries 4 and the backward residual equals the flow. Residual A to T is 2. The path S, B, A, T uses that backward edge. Its bottleneck is the minimum of 4, 4, and 2, which is 2. Augment by 2. Increase f of S B from 6 to 8. Decrease f of A B from 4 to 2. Increase f of A T from 6 to 8. The value rises from 16 to 18. Check A. Inflow from S is 10. Outflow is 8 to T plus 2 to B, which is 10. Check B. Inflow is 8 from S plus 2 from A, which is 10. Outflow to T is 10. Into T, 8 plus 10 equals 18. Out of S, 10 plus 8 equals 18. This maximum flow uses the decoy. The earlier maximum flow did not. Both have value 18. Both saturate A to T and B to T. The cut, not the decoy's number, is what the two assignments share.

Change one capacity and recompute, so the 18 is not a memorized label. Let the capacity of B to T fall from 10 to 6, and leave the other capacities alone. The cut into T now has capacity 8 plus 6, which is 14. Run the same shortest-path habit. First path S, A, T, bottleneck the minimum of 10 and 8, which is 8. Value 8. Residual B to T is still 6, and S to B is 10, so the next shortest path is S, B, T with bottleneck 6. Value 14. After that, B to T is saturated, A to T is saturated, and T is unreachable even though S to A still has residual 2 and A to B still has residual 4. The reachable source side is again S, A, and B. The cut capacity is 14, and the flow value is 14. Lowering one cut edge lowered the optimum by the same amount, from 18 to 14. The decoy still did not have to move. If instead you had lowered an edge that is not in the minimum cut, you would not get to assume the optimum moves. The decoy's capacity can change without moving this particular cut's capacity at all, as long as the cut you are pricing is still the one into T.

You might ask whether you are required to return the paths, or whether the minimum cut is the answer the caller wanted. The theorem certifies a number, the maximum value. It also hands you one source side, the reachable set, whose capacity equals that number. It does not hand you a unique flow. You just built two different assignments with the same value. If a caller wants a routing, you still have a flow in hand, the edge values, and more than one of them can be correct. If a caller wants a proof that no larger value exists, the paths are the wrong object to wave around. Another path might be hiding in a residual you did not draw. The cut is the object that makes hiding impossible. Anything larger than 18 would have to cross from the reachable side to T, and the only crossing capacities are 8 and 10.

Here is what the equality does not mean. It does not mean every cut has capacity 18. You priced cuts of 20, 22, and 20. It does not mean every saturated edge belongs to the minimum cut. In the second maximum flow, S to A carries 10 and is saturated, and the cut consisting of only S has capacity 20, not 18. Saturation is necessary for edges that leave a minimum cut, in the sense that a minimum cut's forward edges are full when the flow is maximum, and you saw A to T and B to T full in both maximum flows. Saturation is not a test you can apply to a single edge in isolation. It does not mean an idle edge was a mistake in the input. A to B can stay at 0, and the lab says the value should read 18 with that idle edge allowed. It does not mean you should trust the theorem as a proverb while skipping the residual search. The reachable set is defined by that search. If you name a source side that is not exactly the reachable set, you may name a heavier cut and then wonder why its capacity is larger than the flow. The proof you trust is the equality between the flow you pushed and the capacity of the cut the failed search defines. On this graph that equality reads 18 equals 18. That is the end of the argument, and it is the whole argument.
