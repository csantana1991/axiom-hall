Hot and cold are classifications of work, not of prestige. Hot, on the slide, is parse, decide, send, or hand off. Cold is train, explain, or write the model card. Do not put a cold job in the hot budget except as a forbidden line. A forbidden line is a line you keep visible so you can see that it must stay at zero on this path. It is not a line you fill with a training time and then call the path interactive. Training is cold. The hot budget does not contain it. If a training job can stall a send, you have put a cold job on the hot path, and the budget should show that stall as a stage that blocks, usually by showing a design you do not want.

The ring is the handoff, and the lecture is blunt about what it does. It moves a market event. It does not score a model. A lock-free ring can be the right implementation of a handoff and still lose the latency budget to a risk check that parses JSON on the send path. The parse is on the path. The ring is on the path. The parse does work that grows with the document. The ring moves an event that is already in memory. You do not need a cycle count the lecture does not give you to accept the ordering in the sentence. The JSON parse dominates the ring. If your hot path includes that parse, the interesting budget line is the parse, not the ring. Making the ring faster does not move a sum whose large term is the parser.

The notes name the capstone budget as five stages: gateway read, ring push, strategy decision, ring pop, gateway write. If the strategy is a neural net, it will eat the budget. You have two honest exits, and the lecture states both. Either the net is not on this path, or the nanosecond claim is about the ring alone and you say that. A nanosecond claim about the whole path, while a neural net sits in the strategy decision, is a claim the note tells you not to make. The ring's speed and the path's speed are different subjects. Say which one you measured. Features that are not needed for the next order wait. They are cold relative to this handoff, even if some other system needs them soon. Waiting is the correct placement. Computing them before the push, on the hot path, adds a stage the order did not need.

Use the order-of-magnitude arithmetic on this list. Let the gateway read have mean g. Suppose the strategy decision is the model and you set it an order of magnitude above that gateway read, so its mean is ten g. Ring push, ring pop, and gateway write are still unnamed as numbers. Call their means a, b, and c. They are nonnegative. The sum of the means is g plus a plus ten g plus b plus c, which is eleven g plus a plus b plus c. The strategy's share is ten g divided by that sum. The share is less than ten elevenths whenever a, b, or c is positive, and it equals ten elevenths only if the ring and the gateway write have mean zero, which is a strong claim. You can still see dominance without those means. The strategy alone is ten gateway-reads. The sum is at least eleven g. No positive a, b, or c can make the strategy smaller than the gateway read. It stays an order of magnitude above that one stage. If a, b, and c are themselves on the scale of g, the sum is fourteen g and the share is ten fourteenths, which is five sevenths, about zero point seven one four. That seventy-one percent is what "eat the budget" looks like when the other four stages match the gateway read. The lecture did not assert that they match. It asserted that a neural net on this path eats the budget. The arithmetic shows you the share under a stated scale. Replace the scale before you defend it. The lab defaults are invented teaching values.

Change one input. Leave the strategy at ten g and give the JSON parse its own mean of ten g, split out of the gateway write, and keep a gateway write of g beside it. List the means and add them once. Gateway read g, ring push g, strategy ten g, ring pop g, JSON parse ten g, gateway write g. One plus one plus ten plus one plus ten plus one is twenty-four. The sum is twenty-four g. The strategy is no longer the only eater. The parse is just as large. Together they are twenty g out of twenty-four, five sixths of the mean. The ring push is one twenty-fourth. Making the ring lock-free does not move a budget whose mean is five sixths parse and net. That is the sentence about JSON, with a factor of ten attached so the domination is a quotient and not an adjective.

You might ask whether a boring hot path is an admission that the system is simple. It is an admission that the hot path does not do cold work. Simple is the wrong word. A lock-free ring, a gateway read, a gateway write, and a decision that is not a neural net can still be a careful design. The lecture calls it boring on purpose, against the temptation to put a model on the send. Boring means the stages are parse, decide, send, or hand off, and the decide is cheap enough that you are willing to put its mean on the page next to the ring. The capstone ring moves a market event. If your decide scores a model, you are in the other branch of the note: say that the nanosecond claim is about the ring alone, or take the net off this path.

What the claim does not mean is that lock-free structures are slow, or that JSON is forbidden everywhere. JSON on the send path dominates the ring in this lecture's ordering. JSON off the path, in a cold explanation or a log you do not wait on, is a different placement. It does not mean the five capstone names are milliseconds you can quote. The notes give the names and withhold the defended numbers. The defaults in the lab are teaching values. It does not mean a forbidden line is a moral label on training. Training is cold because it is not required to move the next event. It can be the most important job in the building and still be the wrong job to add into the hot sum. It does not mean features are useless. Features not needed for the next order wait. Features needed for the next order are part of the decision, and they belong in that stage's mean, where they can eat the budget honestly.
