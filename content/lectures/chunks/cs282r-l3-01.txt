One more construction, because label alignment is where this hides even when every column looks old. Suppose the feature row is stamped at time two and truly uses only times zero, one, and two. The label beside it in the file was computed at time one, from the jump that ends at time two. Then the feature's current mid, ten at time two, is inside the previous label, and you have aligned a feature with a label that already contains it. The row looks causal if you only inspect the feature code. It is not causal under the join you actually trained. Write the timestamp of the feature and the timestamp of the decision the label belongs to. If the feature timestamp is later than the decision timestamp, the join is the leak. This is still label construction, not a new category. It is the reason a column of clean lags can be guilty after a bad merge.

The question that comes up is whether a centered smoother is harmless when you use it only to draw a chart, and the fit itself is causal. A chart that is not an input is not a feature. You may draw it, and you should label it as a view that uses the future, so nobody reads the smoothness as something a trader could have seen. The moment a number from that chart enters the design, even as a reason you chose a window, you are back in the leakage. "I only used it to decide the width, and then I refit causally" is a use. The width is then a function of the future chart. If the width is chosen on the training block alone, with a causal smoother, the objection expires. Guilty until proven causal is a placement of the burden, not a ban on averages.

What the claim does not mean is that every feature which correlates with the next return is leaked. A legal feature can still move with the label by ordinary dependence. Dependence is not a side door. The side door is a timestamp. If every input timestamp is at or before the decision, and every fitting choice that touches the inputs was made without the test block, a high correlation is a result you may then fail to trust for other reasons. It is not automatically leakage. The claim also does not mean that the label should be censored so that it cannot see the next mid. Starve the label and you have no supervision. Starve the feature of the next mid and you have a chance of an honest one.

The idea that replaces the random cut is walk-forward. Train on the half-open interval from T zero to T one, test on the half-open interval from T one to T two, and roll forward. Do not tune on the test window and then report it. The purge and the embargo around the cut exist because labels overlap the boundary. López de Prado's work is the long version. The short version is that the cut is part of the model. One honest window beats twelve tuned ones.

Read the intervals as inequalities so the endpoint cannot smuggle a day across the cut. A day t is in training when T zero is less than or equal to t and t is strictly less than T one. A day t is in test when T one is less than or equal to t and t is strictly less than T two. The test starts where the training ends. There is no shared day. Roll forward means the next experiment builds a later pair, with a later T one and a later T two, and the previous test block is not quietly promoted into a place where you mine it for the story you tell about the new test block. You may, in a later roll, train on days that were once future relative to an earlier cut. That is allowed because, at the later decision, those days are past. It is not allowed to use them as past before they were past.

Put numbers on one cut. Let T zero be day zero, T one be day one hundred, and T two be day one hundred twenty. Training holds one hundred days, indexes zero through ninety-nine. Testing holds twenty days, indexes one hundred through one hundred nineteen. Nothing about these lengths is sacred. They are a worked case so the embargo has something to bite. Suppose the label horizon is five days. A decision on day ninety-six has a label that looks five days ahead, through day one hundred one. Day one hundred and day one hundred one sit in the test interval. The training row for day ninety-six is therefore built with labels that overlap the test block. The fit sees a function of test-period prices, attached to a row you called training. That is the overlap the purge and the embargo are there to break.

Embargo at least that horizon means the gap you refuse to learn across is at least five days in this example, not a token one-day skip you hope is polite. One concrete way to honor the sentence is to stop training decisions at day one hundred minus five, so the last training decision is on day ninety-five, and its label, reaching five days ahead, ends on day one hundred, which is the first test day. Whether the end is inclusive depends on how you defined the horizon, so write the horizon down before you subtract. If the label uses prices strictly after the decision and up through five days after, a decision on day ninety-five touches day one hundred. If day one hundred is test, you have still touched it, and the embargo has to move one day earlier, to day ninety-four, whose five-day reach ends at day ninety-nine, the last training day. The arithmetic is fussy because the boundary is fussy. The fuss is the model. A person who writes "embargo of five" and then cannot say whether day ninety-five is in or out has not chosen an embargo. They have chosen a word.

Roll the same design forward once, so the verb "roll" is not decorative. The next cut takes T zero still at day zero, or at some later restart you declare, T one at day one hundred twenty, and T two at day one hundred forty. Test is now days one hundred twenty through one hundred thirty-nine. The old test days, one hundred through one hundred nineteen, can enter training for this new cut, because they are past relative to day one hundred twenty. They cannot enter the score you already reported for the first cut. That score was sealed when you looked at it, or it was not a test. And the embargo moves with the cut. The last legal training decision is again at least a horizon before day one hundred twenty. You recompute the subtraction at the new boundary. You do not copy the old row indexes and feel that the embargo is a property of the dataset rather than of the cut.

Here is the twelve-window failure, counted rather than scolded. Suppose you build twelve walk-forward tests, each honest in isolation, and each produces a score. You look at the twelve scores and you put the largest one in the abstract. You have used the test windows as a search space. There were twelve chances to be pleased, and you reported the pleasure. One honest window beats twelve tuned ones means you would rather publish a single predeclared cut than a maximum over a dozen cuts you ranked after seeing them. If you want twelve cuts, report the twelve, or report a rule for aggregating them that you wrote before you saw them. The maximum is a different statistic. It is biased upward by the selection, and the selection is tuning.
