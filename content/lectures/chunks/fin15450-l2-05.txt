
Work one more path in two exact steps, because a single annual draw hides the way the integral adds. Keep S at 0 equal to 100, mu equal to 0.1, and sigma equal to 0.2. Split the year into two half-years, so each width is 0.5 and the square root of the width is the square root of one half, about 0.7071. The log drift per unit time is still 0.08, so each half-year contributes 0.08 times 0.5, which is 0.04, to the exponent, before the noise. Suppose the first standard normal is 1 and the second is negative 0.5. The first Wiener increment is 0.7071 times 1, about 0.7071. The noise piece in the exponent is sigma times that increment, 0.2 times 0.7071, about 0.1414. The first exponent is 0.04 plus 0.1414, about 0.1814. The exponential of 0.1814 is about 1.1989. The level after the first half-year is about 119.892. The second Wiener increment is 0.7071 times negative 0.5, about negative 0.3536. The noise piece is 0.2 times negative 0.3536, about negative 0.0707. The second exponent is 0.04 plus negative 0.0707, about negative 0.0307. The exponential of negative 0.0307 is about 0.9698. Multiply 119.892 by 0.9698 and the level at the end of the year is about 116.266.

Check that product against the closed form on the whole year, which is the point of having an integral rather than a ritual of steps. The total Wiener increment is 0.7071 plus negative 0.3536, about 0.3535. The year-long exponent is 0.08 plus 0.2 times 0.3535, which is 0.08 plus about 0.0707, about 0.1507. The exponential of 0.1507 is about 1.1627, and 100 times 1.1627 is about 116.27. The two half-steps and the one year formula agree. They agree because the exponents add and the Wiener increments add, which is what constant coefficients allow. If a lab step and the closed form disagree on the same normals, the step is not implementing this integral.

The Euler update on the same two halves does not agree, and the gap is the discretization, not a new model. Start at 100. The first Euler increment is mu S dt plus sigma S dW, which is 0.1 times 100 times 0.5, plus 0.2 times 100 times 0.7071, which is 5 plus 14.142, which is 19.142. The level becomes 119.142, against the exact 119.892. The second Euler increment uses the new level. Drift piece: 0.1 times 119.142 times 0.5, about 5.957. Diffusion piece: 0.2 times 119.142 times negative 0.3536, about negative 8.425. The net Euler change is about negative 2.468, and the ending level is about 116.675. The exact ending level was about 116.266. On this particular pair of draws the year-end gap is only about four tenths of a point, while the half-year gap was about three quarters of a point. Gaps can shrink or grow depending on the path. The exact method does not need the gap. The Euler method is a different recursion, accurate at these parameters to a fraction of a percent on this one path and not identical.

This path ended near 116.3. The mean at t equal to 1 is about 110.5. One path sitting above the mean is not a bias in the formula and not a failure of the half-step arithmetic. W ended near 0.35, which is a positive draw, and the exponential turns a positive draw into a level above the W equals 0 center, which was about 108.3. The path at 116.3 is above both the center and the mean. A single path is allowed to be. The mean is not a magnet that each skeleton must approach. It is an average over the Gaussian measure on W. Your one W was 0.35. That is the entire explanation of 116.3.

Set mu to 0 and recompute the three reference levels, so a zero drift cannot be confused with a zero log drift. Sigma stays 0.2 and t stays 1 and S at 0 stays 100. The log drift is 0 minus 0.02, which is negative 0.02. The W equals 0 level is 100 times the exponential of negative 0.02, about 100 times 0.9802, about 98.020. The W equals 1 level is 100 times the exponential of negative 0.02 plus 0.2, the exponential of 0.18, about 119.722. The W equals negative 1 level is 100 times the exponential of negative 0.22. The exponential of negative 0.22 is about 0.8025, so the level is about 80.252. The average of these three pictures is 80.252 plus 98.020 plus 119.722, which is 297.994, divided by 3, about 99.331. The true mean, S at 0 times e to the mu t, is 100 times e to the 0, which is 100. The three-picture average came out about 99.3, close to 100 and not equal to it, and the center 98.020 is visibly below 100. With mu equal to 0 the expected price does not move, and the typical log path still decays at one half sigma squared. If a desk tells you a zero expected return means the fan is centered at the starting price, they are describing the mean and not the W equals 0 path. The W equals 0 path is at about 98.02. The mean is 100. Both sentences fit in the same model because the right tail, the W equals 1 picture at about 119.7 against a left tail at about 80.3, is asymmetric.

Shorten the horizon instead of the drift. Return mu to 0.1 and sigma to 0.2, and set t equal to 0.5. The mean is 100 times e to the 0.05. The exponential of 0.05 is about 1.05127, so the mean is about 105.127. The log contribution is 0.08 times 0.5, which is 0.04, and e to the 0.04 is about 1.04081, so the W equals 0 level is about 104.081. The gap has shrunk to about 1.046, from about 2.188 on the full year, which is what you expect when both the mean exponent and the variance of the log are proportional to t. Half the time, a smaller Jensen gap. None of this required a new theorem. It is the same pair of expressions, S at 0 times e to the mu t, and S at 0 times e to the quantity mu minus one half sigma squared, times t, evaluated at a different t.

Take the partial test to one more awkward price so the cancellation is not a trick of round hundreds. Let S equal 7 and sigma equal 0.1. One half sigma squared is 0.005. One half sigma squared S squared is 0.005 times 49, which is 0.245. The second derivative of the log is negative 1 over 49. The product is 0.245 times negative 1 over 49. But 0.245 is 0.005 times 49, so the product is 0.005 times 49 times negative 1 over 49, which is negative 0.005. The 49 cancels. Negative one half sigma squared is negative 0.005. If a leftover factor of 7, or of 49, is sitting in your correction, you differentiated 1 over S into something other than negative 1 over S squared, or you multiplied by sigma S rather than by one half sigma squared S squared. The notes are blunt about this because the algebra is short enough to audit. Write every partial. Multiply. Watch the price disappear. Then, and only then, integrate.

The audit leaves you with three numbers you should not mix when you look at the lab. The skeleton is S at 0 times the exponential of the quantity mu minus one half sigma squared, times t, plus sigma times the realized W. On the two-step year that number was about 116.3 for one particular W. The mean is S at 0 times e to the mu t, about 110.5 on the base parameters for one year, about 105.1 for a half year, and exactly the starting price when mu is 0. The exponential of the expected log is the lower one, about 108.3, about 104.1, and about 98.0 in those same three settings. The fan illustrates the skeleton. The closed-form mean is the middle sentence. A call price, when you meet it, will be none of these three, and averaging a few skeletons will not produce it. If the fan and the mean disagree, that is the model working. If the exact step and the closed form disagree on the same W, that is a bug.
