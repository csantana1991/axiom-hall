Change one input. Lift eight instead of five, still from the ten-lot ask, still against the decision mid of one hundred. Eight is still inside the level, so the price you pay per share is still one hundred point zero two. The per-share shortfall is still two cents. It does not become larger just because you traded more shares inside the same price. The total shortfall is eight times two cents, which is sixteen cents, before fees. The remaining ask size is two, not five. The per-share number stayed. The total changed. The book after the trade changed. If you report only "two cents" you have reported the per-share gap and hidden the scale. If you report only "sixteen cents" you have reported the total and hidden that the price gap per share was the same two cents the note computed for five. Write both, and write the remaining size, because the note's sentence for the five-lot case carried all three: two cents a share, on those five, remaining ask size five.

Now change a different single input, the decision price, and leave the fill alone. Suppose you wrote down an arrival price of ninety-nine point nine eight, the bid, instead of the mid, and you still lift five at one hundred point zero two. The fill does not change. The benchmark does. The gap is one hundred point zero two minus ninety-nine point nine eight, which is four cents a share, the whole spread, not the half-spread. Five times four cents is twenty cents, before fees. Same trade, different decision price, different shortfall. This is why the benchmark is part of the definition and not a caption you add later. Arrival price is a common choice. The mid is the choice the note used. They agree on this particular touch only in the sense that the note's decision mid equals the touch mid. They are not synonyms in the definition. The definition is: the price you wrote down before the trade, versus the average fill.

You might ask whether shortfall is just the half-spread. On the note's own case, yes, the per-share shortfall equals the half-spread, and the reason is narrow. You lifted the ask, you did not walk, and the decision price was the mid. Two cents equals two cents for those reasons. Lift more than the ten lots and the average fill is worse than one hundred point zero two, so the per-share shortfall exceeds two cents, once a worse price exists. The notes do not give the worse price, so you stop at the inequality and you do not invent the excess. Choose a decision price that is not the mid, as in the arrival at the bid, and the per-share shortfall is four cents, not two. The half-spread is a property of the touch. Shortfall is a property of a decision price and a fill. They met in one example. The meeting is not the definition.

What the claim does not mean should be said before anyone treats the ten cents as a research result. It does not mean you have a distribution. A single fill in the lab is a demonstration, not a distribution. One ten-cent total is not an average shortfall, not a quantile, and not a fact about the next click. It does not mean the number is implementation shortfall in a calibrated model of the whole market. It is decision price versus average fill on this take, before fees. It does not mean the seller of those five lots booked a ten-cent loss. Shortfall is marked from your decision price. The other side has a different decision, or none you know. It does not mean you may choose the benchmark after you see the fill. If you declare the fill itself to be the decision price, the gap is zero by construction. That zero is a story. It is not a shortfall. It does not mean fees were zero in the market. It means this paragraph stopped before fees, because no fee was given. It does not mean the remaining size is irrelevant to the demonstration. The note tells you to see that the remaining ask is five and that imbalance moves. Those are checks that your hand calculation matches the book. They are not extra dollars added to the ten cents.

Do the hand check in the order the lab brief asks, so the demonstration has a sequence. Before the click, write the mid. On this touch it is one hundred. Write the imbalance. If you have supposed a bid size of ten, write zero. If you have not been given a bid size, write the expression in q, and do not pretend you observed a decimal. Then lift five. Recompute the mid. It is still one hundred, because the best prices did not move. Recompute the imbalance. It is one third under the supposition, or the higher ratio you derived for general q. Recompute the fill and the gap. The fill is one hundred point zero two. The gap is two cents a share, ten cents on five, before fees. Then look. The lab should match the hand. If it does not, one of the two calculations used a different size, a different benchmark, or a walk you did not intend. Matching is the exercise. A prettier number is not.

One last contrast, dump the whole ten-lot ask against the same decision mid, so a full level is not confused with a walk. You lift ten. The level empties. Every one of the ten lots prints at one hundred point zero two, because the entire take fit on that level. There is no remainder to send to a worse price. The average fill is still one hundred point zero two. Per-share shortfall against the mid is still two cents. Total shortfall is ten times two cents, which is twenty cents, before fees. Remaining size at that price is zero, and the lab deletes the level. What the next best ask is, you have not been told. The shortfall on these ten lots does not depend on that next price, because none of these ten lots printed there. The book after the deletion does depend on it. Shortfall is about the fill you got. The new touch is about the state you left. Keep those ledgers apart, or you will start adding a spread you have not observed into a cost you have already closed.
