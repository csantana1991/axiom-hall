A small version makes the bias easy to see without a probability lecture. Suppose the twelve scores, as an illustration and not as a lab result, are zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, zero, and one. Eleven windows say zero. One window says one. The honest description is that the predeclared window, whichever it was, has its own number, and that the set of twelve contains a one. The tuned description says the method scored one. Those sentences describe different procedures. The cut is part of the model because changing which window you are willing to call "the" result changes the procedure. López de Prado's work is the long version of purge, embargo, and the overlapping-label problem. You are not being asked, in this room, to recite the long version from memory. You are being asked to keep the short version in the fit: the cut is part of the model, and a label that crosses it is not a training label.

Do the subtraction on a horizon that is not five, so the number five does not become a superstition. Let the cut stay at day one hundred, and let the label horizon be one day, where the label uses the next day's price and no further. A decision on day ninety-nine has a label that touches day one hundred. Day one hundred is the first test day. If touching day one hundred is forbidden, the last training decision is day ninety-eight, whose next day is ninety-nine, still inside training. The embargo is at least one day, and the off-by-one of the endpoint can make it two steps of index, from ninety-nine down to ninety-eight, depending on whether the horizon's last day is inclusive. Write that down. A ten-day horizon on the same cut pushes the last safe decision much earlier. If the label on day t uses days t plus one through t plus ten, then t plus ten must be at most ninety-nine, so t is at most eighty-nine. Day eighty-nine's label ends on day ninety-nine. Day ninety's label ends on day one hundred, which is test, so day ninety is out. The excluded training decisions are those t where t plus the horizon reaches the test start. With horizon ten and test start one hundred, t plus ten is at least one hundred when t is at least ninety. Inside a training region that would otherwise have ended at ninety-nine, the excluded decisions are ninety through ninety-nine. That is ten decisions. The embargo is at least the horizon, and under this endpoint convention the horizon and the number of excluded edge decisions match. They will not match under every convention, which is why you write the convention rather than memorizing ten.

If you roll twelve cuts and the embargo is wrong on each of them, you do not have twelve honest windows with a selection problem. You have twelve leaked windows, and selecting the best of them stacks a second failure on the first. Repair the embargo before you talk about honesty across windows. One clean cut with the ten edge decisions removed is the object the short version is pointing at. Twelve pretty numbers with the edge left in are not made honest by being numerous.

The question to answer is how long the embargo has to be. At least the label horizon. Not at most the horizon, and not a fixed courtesy gap that ignores the horizon you actually used. If the label looks ten days out, an embargo of five is short, and the rows near the cut still contain the test. If the label looks one day out, an embargo of one day is the minimum the sentence allows, and a longer embargo is a further choice you should name rather than a contradiction. If two labels are in the same model, with horizons five and ten, the embargo is at least the longer one, otherwise the longer label still reaches across. Write the horizon next to the cut. Then the subtraction is determined. If you cannot point to the horizon, you cannot point to the embargo, and you are not ready to report the test number.

What the claim does not mean is that a walk-forward split makes the model good, or that an embargo repairs a future mid hiding inside a feature far from the boundary. The split orders the blocks. The embargo protects the boundary against labels that hang off the edge of a block. A feature that reads tomorrow's mid on every row is leaked in the middle of the training block, where no embargo is looking. You still have to pass the timestamp test from the previous idea. The claim also does not mean that one window is statistically precise. One window can be noisy. The lecture prefers one honest window to twelve tuned ones because honesty and selection bias are the subject, not because a short test has a small variance. You may say the window is thin. You may not thicken it by picking the best of twelve and speaking as if you had only ever planned to run that one.

The idea that keeps the lab from being misquoted is a negative one. You should be able to say what the attention lab is not. It is not a trained transformer, and it does not predict returns. The readout is a weighted sum of the past values, which on this toy equals a smoothed copy of the past. If someone quotes a Sharpe ratio from it, they are joking or they are wrong. The lab has no split because it has no fit. Turn the mask off in your head, not in the lab. Future weights would light up, and the exercise would be cheating. Keep the mask. Write the walk-forward cut you would have used if these six numbers had been a week.

A smoothed copy of the past is a specific object. Take three past values, two, zero, and two, and three weights that sum to one, one half, one quarter, and one quarter. The weighted sum is one half times two, plus one quarter times zero, plus one quarter times two, which is one plus zero plus one half, which is one and one half. Every input to that sum is a past value. The output one and one half sits between them. It does not claim that the next value will be one and one half. If the next value is nine, the smoother is not "wrong" about nine, because it never spoke about nine. It spoke about a weighted view of two, zero, and two. Calling that view a return forecast adds a verb the calculation does not contain.

Flatten the same three values to equal weights and the smoother becomes the ordinary mean. Two plus zero plus two is four, divided by three, which is four thirds. Four thirds is still a function of the past alone. Raising a temperature until the weights nearly match is still not a forecast. It is a more even copy. The lab's six signed imbalance numbers, if you attend causally and you set the value equal to the number itself, produce a number in the same units as those imbalances. A signed imbalance is not a return. Averaging it does not change its unit into a profit.

The checklist before any fit, for the day you do have a fit, is short enough to say in one breath and strict enough to fail a project. Timestamp on every column. Scaler fit on the training block only. Label horizon written down. Embargo at least that horizon. Costs in the score. A baseline that is embarrassingly simple, such as predict zero. If the model cannot beat predict zero after costs, the attention was a picture. The lab you are looking at today does not get to skip the spirit of that list by being small. It gets to skip the split only because there is no fit to split. The moment you fit, the list turns on.
