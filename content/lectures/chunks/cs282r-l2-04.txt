The question to settle is whether a bidirectional encoder is acceptable when you are only exploring, and you promise to be causal later. Exploration that scores a fit using future ticks is not a preview of the causal model. It is a different function. On the first paper row the unmasked output was pulled to about five, and the masked output was about zero point five seven five. Those are not close. If you tune a temperature, a width, or a story on the unmasked number, you are tuning a leak. Saying which one you trained means you write "causal" or "bidirectional" on the result before you write the result. If you trained bidirectional, you do not have a forecaster, and you should not borrow a forecasting verb for it. If you trained causal, you attend only at or before the query index, and you can say that without appealing to the promise you intend to keep next week.

What the claim does not mean is that a mask repairs a bad label, a shuffled split, or a scaler fit on the whole sample. The mask is about which keys enter the weighted average. It is one gate. A future mid hiding in a different column will not be stopped by an attention mask you applied to a different input. The claim also does not mean that every zero weight is a mask. At a low temperature the opposite-sign key had a weight of about zero point zero one six, which is dark to the eye and still a legal past key. Dark because of the softmax is not dark because of negative infinity. Read the index, not the color. The claim also does not mean that including "now" is leakage. The query time is knowable at the query time. The indexes after it are not.

When you leave the lab, the future ticks stay dark because the mask is on. Drop the temperature and the legal weights sharpen onto the past tick whose sign matches the query. Raise the temperature and the legal row flattens. None of those movements light the future. If you want to see the future light up, do it on paper, as you did with the key of five and the weight of about ninety-seven hundredths, and then put the mask back before you trust the picture.
