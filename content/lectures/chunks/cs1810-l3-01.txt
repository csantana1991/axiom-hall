The idea in this block is where the bowl moves. The penalty pulls toward the origin, the gradient gains lambda x and lambda y, and the minimizer leaves the point 1 and negative 0.4. That slide is the bias you bought. You should be able to name the new point without running the iteration, and then you should use the iteration only as a check that the code is aimed at the same point.

Set the new gradient to zero and solve the two coordinates separately. They do not couple. In x, 2 times x minus 1, plus lambda x, equals zero. That is x times the quantity 2 plus lambda, minus 2, equals zero, so x times the quantity 2 plus lambda equals 2, so x equals 2 divided by the quantity 2 plus lambda. In y, 6 times y plus 0.4, plus lambda y, equals zero. That is y times the quantity 6 plus lambda, plus 2.4, equals zero, because 6 times 0.4 is 2.4. So y times the quantity 6 plus lambda equals negative 2.4, and y equals negative 2.4 divided by the quantity 6 plus lambda. The fixed point solves this linear equation, not the old one. The old one was the special case lambda equals 0, which returns x equals 2 over 2, which is 1, and y equals negative 2.4 over 6, which is negative 0.4. The formula includes the unpenalized answer. It does not stop there.

Compute a short table so the slide is a list of points rather than a gesture. At lambda equals 0.5, x equals 2 over 2.5, which is 0.8, and y equals negative 2.4 over 6.5. Divide 2.4 by 6.5. Multiply numerator and denominator by 10, 24 over 65, which is about 0.36923, so y is about negative 0.3692. At lambda equals 1, x equals 2 over 3, about 0.6667, and y equals negative 2.4 over 7, about negative 0.3429. At lambda equals 2, x equals 2 over 4, which is 0.5, and y equals negative 2.4 over 8, which is negative 0.3. At lambda equals 4, x equals 2 over 6, which is one third, and y equals negative 2.4 over 10, which is negative 0.24. At lambda equals 6, x equals 2 over 8, which is 0.25, and y equals negative 2.4 over 12, which is negative 0.2. At lambda equals 10, x equals 2 over 12, which is one sixth, and y equals negative 2.4 over 16, which is negative 0.15. At lambda equals 100, x equals 2 over 102, about 0.0196, and y equals negative 2.4 over 106, about negative 0.0226. Every increase of lambda moves both coordinates toward zero. They do not arrive at zero for any finite lambda. The origin is the limit, not a point you hit with a moderate penalty.

The original bowl at these new points is the training loss you gave up. At lambda equals 2 the point is 0.5 and negative 0.3. The bowl is the square of 0.5 minus 1, plus 3 times the square of negative 0.3 plus 0.4. That is the square of negative 0.5, plus 3 times the square of 0.1, which is 0.25 plus 0.03, which is 0.28. The unpenalized minimum was 0. You paid 0.28 on the original surface. The penalty term at that same point is lambda over 2 times 0.25 plus 0.09, and lambda over 2 is 1, so the penalty term is 0.34. The penalized value is 0.28 plus 0.34, which is 0.62. Earlier, the old minimizer had penalized value 1.16 at this lambda, and the origin had penalized value 1.48. The new point beats both, 0.62 against 1.16 and against 1.48. The purchase is visible as a pair: original bowl up from 0 to 0.28, penalized objective down from 1.16 to 0.62 relative to staying at the old point. If you report only 0.62, you have hidden the 0.28. The lesson's instruction is to report both losses or you are hiding the purchase.

Check stationarity at that point so the formula is not a black box. The x partial is 2 times 0.5 minus 1, plus 2 times 0.5, which is 2 times negative 0.5, plus 1, which is negative 1 plus 1, which is 0. The y partial is 6 times negative 0.3 plus 0.4, plus 2 times negative 0.3, which is 6 times 0.1, plus negative 0.6, which is 0.6 minus 0.6, which is 0. The gradient is the zero vector. The point is not an approximate printout of the old minimizer. It is the exact stationary point of the new function.

Do lambda equals 4 the same way, because a second row is what makes the pattern a method. The point is one third and negative 0.24. The original bowl is the square of one third minus 1, plus 3 times the square of negative 0.24 plus 0.4. One third minus 1 is negative two thirds, and its square is four ninths, about 0.4444. The y error is 0.16, its square is 0.0256, and 3 times 0.0256 is 0.0768. The bowl is about 0.5212. The penalty term is 4 over 2 times the sum of one ninth and 0.0576. One ninth is about 0.1111, plus 0.0576 is about 0.1687, times 2 is about 0.3374. The penalized value is about 0.8587. The original bowl got worse than the 0.28 you paid at lambda equals 2, which is the direction of the bias as lambda grows. At the old minimizer the penalized value would have been 2.32, from the previous block, so 0.8587 still wins on the penalized scale. At the origin the penalized value is still 1.48, and 0.8587 beats that too. Large lambda makes the origin win only as a limit of the location. The value of the penalized function at its own minimizer is climbing toward the origin's bowl value 1.48 from below. At lambda equals 100 the original bowl at the tiny point is about 1.388, the penalty term is about 0.045, and the penalized value is about 1.433, close to 1.48, with the parameter close to the origin and not equal to it.

The iteration is the check, not the derivation. Start at the origin with lambda equals 2 and eta equals 0.1. The ceiling is 0.25, so 0.1 is legal. The gradient at the origin is still negative 2 and 2.4, because the penalty terms lambda x and lambda y are zero there. The first landing is 0.2 and negative 0.24, the same first landing as the unpenalized walk. The second step is where the penalty becomes visible. At 0.2 and negative 0.24 the x partial is 2 times negative 0.8, plus 2 times 0.2, which is negative 1.6 plus 0.4, which is negative 1.2. The y partial is 6 times 0.16, plus 2 times negative 0.24, which is 0.96 minus 0.48, which is 0.48. Subtract 0.1 times that gradient. x goes to 0.2 plus 0.12, which is 0.32. y goes to negative 0.24 minus 0.048, which is negative 0.288. Continue, and the later points march toward 0.5 and negative 0.3, not toward 1 and negative 0.4. After a handful of steps you are near 0.49 and negative 0.3. If your late iterates sit near 0.5 and negative 0.3, the code and the formula agree. If they sit near 1 and negative 0.4, you are still minimizing the old function, which means the penalty is not in the update. Those are different bugs. A path that is still moving has not failed the formula yet. A path that has settled in the wrong place has.
