A tiny decide has a ratio you should also be able to flip. Fifty nanoseconds of decide against two hundred fifty nanoseconds of handoff means the handoff is five times the decide. If parse and send are unknown, you still do not add invented values for them. You say the decide is a small neighbor of the handoff only if you measured the decide, and otherwise you say you have not measured it. Five times is the arithmetic of the supposition. It becomes a slide only when the fifty is real. Until then the first slide carries the choice of sentence, not the number five.

The question to answer is where a model is allowed to sit if the ring is the capstone. It is allowed to sit off the path you are timing, or it is allowed to sit on the path if the decide is tiny and you say so. It is not allowed to sit on the path, take milliseconds, and then vanish from the sentence that quotes the ring. The ring moves an event from a producer to a consumer. If the consumer is a model, the consumer's runtime is the consumer's runtime. You can report it. You cannot report it as nanoseconds you did not measure, and you cannot report the ring's hypothetical nanoseconds as if they included it. Isolation means the model is a different line on the slide, possibly a line that says you have not measured it. A missing line is more honest than a line that hides the model inside the handoff.

What the claim does not mean is that a slow strategy is a failed capstone. The capstone is the handoff, the checksum, and a budget that tells the truth about whether the strategy is on the path. A huge strategy mean means the nanosecond target is about a different path. It does not mean you have failed the ring. It means you stop advertising the ring's ambition as the latency of the strategy. The claim also does not mean that parse, decide, and send is a slogan you can satisfy by typing those three words above a network diagram. Tiny is a comparison. Fifty nanoseconds beside two hundred fifty is a different story from fifty thousand beside two hundred fifty. If you have not measured the decide, you do not get to call it tiny. You write the sentence in the form that admits the missing measurement, and you leave the nanosecond box for a later hour, when a machine has earned it.

When you open the lab for this hour, you are looking at the engine's budget, with teaching numbers, not at a leaderboard. Make the strategy stage honest before you open the ring. If the strategy mean is huge, say that the two-hundred-fifty-nanosecond ambition is about a different path, and say whether you are reporting the ring in isolation. That is the first slide. The rest of the practicum is a handoff you can still explain after the slide has told the truth.
