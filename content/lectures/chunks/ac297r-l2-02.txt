Check the arithmetic of "how many are inside" without storing it, on two of the states you already trust. After the four initial pushes, head is four and tail is zero. Four minus zero is four, the capacity, and the fifth push failed. After the full drain, head is five and tail is five. Five minus five is zero, and the extra pop failed. After the first pop and before the extra push, head was four and tail was one. Four minus one is three. You had pushed four and popped one, and three were left. The subtraction of the two monotonic counters agrees with the history. It is a check you can do on paper. It is not a field you must place on the ring and share. If your paper tickets disagree with the lab, believe the lab and find the off-by-one, then come back and see whether head minus tail still matches the number of successful pushes minus the number of successful pops.

Do the capacity-sixteen case at the joints only, because the outcome you are preparing is a sixteen-slot ring, and the notes' capacity of four should not be the only scale your hand can manage. Cells zero through fifteen start with sequences equal to their indexes. Head and tail start at zero. Sixteen successful pushes bring the head to sixteen. Each push i, for i from zero through fifteen, writes slot i and publishes sequence i plus one. The seventeenth push loads head sixteen, masks to slot zero, and acquires sequence one. One minus sixteen is negative. Difference less than zero. Full. The push returns false. Pop once from tail zero. Slot zero's sequence is one, which equals tail plus one. Claim the tail to one. Publish zero plus sixteen, which is sixteen. Push again. Head sixteen matches sequence sixteen. Claim the head to seventeen. Publish seventeen. The ticket on slot zero is seventeen, not zero. Seventeen is one plus sixteen, the first publish plus the capacity. The same sentence as the capacity-four walk, where the ticket became five, which was one plus four. If you reset slot zero to zero on the pop, the producer waiting for sixteen would see a negative difference and the ring would refuse the next lap. Forget the capacity publish and the ring works once.

Lay the occupancy beside the tickets at five moments so the subtraction is a habit rather than a trick you used once. Moment one, the start. Head zero, tail zero, difference zero. Empty, and the consumer's ticket test agrees. Moment two, after one push. Head one, tail zero, difference one. One event lives in slot zero, whose ticket is one. Moment three, after four pushes. Head four, tail zero, difference four. Full. The fifth push returns false, which agrees with a difference equal to the capacity. Moment four, after one pop. Head four, tail one, difference three. Moment five, after the extra push and the full drain. Head five, tail five, difference zero. Empty again, and a further pop returns false. Five moments, one subtraction, and at no moment did you need a size field to know the count. If you insert a size field and it ever disagrees with head minus tail, the size field is the bug. The tickets and the monotonic counters are the protocol. The size field would be a second source of truth, which is a polite name for a source of lies.

The capacity-sixteen joints obey the same subtraction. After sixteen pushes, head sixteen minus tail zero is sixteen, and the seventeenth push fails. After one pop, head sixteen minus tail one is fifteen. After the extra push, head seventeen minus tail one is sixteen, and the ring is full again, with slot zero holding ticket seventeen. After a complete drain of those seventeen events, if you popped once before the extra push and sixteen more times to catch up, tail would meet head at seventeen, and the difference would be zero. You do not have to recite all sixteen intermediate tickets to see the endpoints. You do have to be able to compute one of them if asked. Slot three, for example, starts at sequence three. The producer publishes four when head is three. Much later the consumer, when tail is three, publishes three plus sixteen, which is nineteen. The next producer publish on that slot is the head that lands there, which is nineteen, plus one, which is twenty. Twenty is four plus sixteen. Again the ticket advanced by the capacity between producer publishes, and it did not reset to three or to zero. If your drawing of slot three shows a zero on the second lap, the drawing is the reset bug, moved from slot zero to a slot whose starting index is not zero. The bug is easier to see on slot three precisely because a reset to zero is obviously not the starting ticket and obviously not the next lap.

The question here is whether you may keep a size field anyway, off to the side, just for logging, as long as the protocol does not branch on it. A log that is not on the ring, written by one thread from values it already owns, is a log. A size field inside the ring object, updated by both sides, is another shared counter even if you promise not to branch on it. The promise does not change the cache line. You do not need it for correctness, because head and tail and the cell tickets already tell you full, empty, and, on paper, the occupancy. If a debugger wants a count, compute it from a snapshot you understand to be racy, or have the consumer count pops locally. Do not add a shared size to make the whiteboard feel more like a queue from an introductory course. This queue tells the truth with tickets.

What the claim does not mean is that the same masked index is a bug, or that full and empty are indistinguishable. They are distinguishable, and the whole point of the ticket is that they remain distinguishable when the indexes coincide. At the start, slot zero meant empty. After four pushes, slot zero meant full. After the drain, slot one meant empty, while a producer would still be allowed to write it. If your test treats "indexes equal" as "empty," you will call a full ring empty and then overflow it. If your test treats "indexes equal" as "full," you will refuse to push into an empty ring at startup, because the indexes are equal on the first line of the program. The difference less than zero is the test, and it is a different difference for each role. The claim also does not mean that returning false is a drop you should retry inside the protocol until it becomes true. False means not now. A caller may retry. The ring may not spin inside the definition of a successful push and call the spin a transfer.

The idea that people try to delete, once they have followed a single producer around the ring, is the compare-exchange. Compare-exchange weak can fail even when the value matched, so the loop is mandatory. With one producer the compare-exchange is logically redundant. It stays in the listing because this is the general cell protocol, specialized to one writer by convention. Do not add a second producer and hope the comment saves you.
