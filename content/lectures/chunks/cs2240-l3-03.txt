Check the diminishing-returns definition on the same sets, so submodular is not only a label. Adding C to the empty collection covers one new point. Adding C after D covers zero new points, because D already holds four. One down to zero is helping less as the set grows. Adding A to the empty collection covers two. Adding A after D covers one. Two down to one is the same pattern. A function that helped more after the set grew would violate the definition you were given, and the factor would not be yours to quote. You do not need a second example to remember the direction. Helps less, or at any rate not more. The coverage counts go the right way. A story that goes the wrong way is not rescued by a citation you do not have.

The paragraph stays one paragraph in spirit even after the arithmetic, which is the lecture’s request. You are not building a trading system out of coverage sets in this studio. You are preventing a particular kind of inflation, the kind that hears a diminishing return and emits a constant. The constant is real. The hypotheses are real. The lab you are sitting in front of is still a frequency sketch. Use it as a frequency instrument only. If the question on the table is a latency quantile, the previous beat already gave you the sentence: histogram with edges you show, or a bounded rank sketch, and the name of which one. If the question on the table is which k sets to pick under a monotone submodular coverage function, greedy and the factor are the relevant theorem, and you still have to be inside the hypotheses. Most questions are neither, and then neither slogan applies.

One last numeric refusal, so the desk story dies in arithmetic rather than in tone. Someone says a second signal improved a book by half as much as the first, so the book is submodular and greedy is within sixty-three hundredths of optimal. You have one sentence of marginal gains, no set function, no proof of monotonicity, no cardinality k, and no greedy that selected a best element. Half as much is a ratio of two increments. It is compatible with submodularity and also compatible with a function that later increases its marginal gains. Two increments cannot certify the whole function. They cannot license the factor. If the optimum were ten, the floor would be a little more than six and three tenths only after the hypotheses are met. They are not met by the sentence. You say so, and you go back to the instrument you actually have, which estimates a point frequency from nonnegative updates, or to the histogram whose edges you are willing to write down.

The three beats of this lesson are three refusals to mix tools. A median is a rank, and a count-min grid does not compute it. Space for a latency question is either edges you chose or a rank sketch you named, not an unbounded vector and not a JavaScript timing dressed up as nanosecond quantiles. A submodular guarantee is one minus one over e under a cardinality constraint and the theorem’s hypotheses, not a mood of diminishing returns. Keep the tools in those lanes and the numbers stay checkable. Cross the lanes and the same numbers become costumes.
