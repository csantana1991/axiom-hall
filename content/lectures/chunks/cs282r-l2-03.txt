The question that belongs to this calculation is why the lab bothers with temperature when the famous formula divides by the square root of d. Because d equals one, and the square root of d is a yawn. The division by the square root of d is the scale that keeps a high-dimensional softmax out of the saturated region. With one-dimensional keys, that scale is one, and you would have no knob. Temperature is the knob. It plays the role of the scale. It does not play the role of a second model. You are not fitting tau on a test set in this lab. You are moving it so you can see sharpening and flattening with your own arithmetic.

What the claim does not mean is that a weighted average of past numbers is a prediction of the next number. You have not looked at a future key yet, and you should not. It also does not mean that the weights are a convolution in disguise. The weights moved when the query changed, and they moved when tau changed, without any edit to a shared kernel. It also does not mean that max-subtraction is a different softmax. The factor cancels. If you subtract the max and your weights change, you have a bug in the division, not a new theory. Finally, equal scores give equal weights. The formula does not contain a quiet preference for the latest tick. If you want that preference, you have to write it down as something the score depends on. Until you do, two matching signs share the row.

The idea that keeps the average honest is the mask. A causal mask sets future scores to negative infinity before the softmax, so a forecast cannot read the ticks it is supposed to predict. The lab's query only attends to indexes at or before now. If you remove that, the pretty fit is leakage. Attend only to j less than or equal to the query index. A bidirectional encoder is not a forecaster. Say which one you trained.

Negative infinity is a calculation, not a mood. Take a score that you set to negative infinity. Subtract the maximum of the scores you are keeping, or, if you are careless, let the negative infinity be the thing you exponentiate directly. e to the power of negative infinity is zero. A zero numerator is a zero weight, as long as the denominator is a sum over the legal indexes and is not zero. The future tick contributes nothing to the weighted sum. The legal ticks are renormalized over themselves. You do not need a second formula. You need to refuse the future index before the exponentials are added.

Use the first paper row and add a future key that would dominate if you let it. Query one, temperature one, past keys one, zero, and negative one, and a future key of five. Unmasked scores are one, zero, negative one, and five. The maximum is five. Centered scores are negative four, negative five, negative six, and zero. The exponentials are about zero point zero one eight three, about zero point zero zero six seven, about zero point zero zero two five, and one. The sum is about one point zero two seven five. The weight on the future key is about zero point nine seven three. The three legal keys share the remaining two point seven hundredths. The output, if the value is the number itself, is almost five, the future key. You have not computed a summary of the past. You have copied the future, with a little blur. That pretty number will fit almost anything whose label is related to the future tick, because you put the future tick in the input. The pretty fit is leakage.

Put the mask back. The future score is negative infinity and its weight is zero. You are back to the three-key row you already computed at temperature one: weights about zero point six six five, zero point two four five, and zero point zero nine zero, and a weighted sum of about zero point five seven five if the values are the keys. The future key of five does not move that sum by a single unit in the last place you are tracking. Future ticks stay dark because the mask is on. The lab brief says to turn the mask off in your head, not in the lab. You just did the head version. The weight that lights up is about ninety-seven hundredths on the future key. Leave the lab's mask on.

Do the same crime on the second paper row, so a tie does not make you feel safe. Query two, past keys two, negative one, zero, and two, future key five, temperature one. Unmasked scores are four, negative two, zero, four, and ten, because two times five is ten. The maximum is ten. The future exponential dominates even more sharply than five did against a query of one, because the product grew. You do not need the full decimal to see the direction. The centered future score is zero, and the next best centered scores are four minus ten, which is negative six. e to the negative six is about zero point zero zero two five, and two of those still sum to about half a hundredth, against a denominator near one. The future weight is above ninety-nine hundredths. Mask it, and you return to the tie you already trust: about zero point four nine five on each of the two matching past keys, and almost nothing on the other two past keys. The mask did not break the tie. It only deleted the index that was not allowed to vote. A bidirectional pass would have let index four, the future, vote, and the tie in the past would have become irrelevant. That is what "a bidirectional encoder is not a forecaster" means on this row.

Say the index rule in the form you can check on six positions. Number the toy sequence zero through five, since the lab has six numbers. Suppose the query index is three, so "now" is the fourth number, counting from one, and indexes four and five are the future. The legal keys are indexes zero, one, two, and three. That is j less than or equal to the query index, four keys, not three. People drop the query's own time when they hear the word past. The rule in the lecture includes now, unless you have written a stricter rule and said so. If your paper row has three weights and the lab has four, the usual culprit is that off-by-one, or a mask that used a strict less-than. Check the inequality before you debug the exponential.

A short count of how much of a centered window is illegal will connect the mask to the leakage you already respect, without doing the whole walk-forward hour. Suppose you are at index three in a sequence of six, and someone proposes a window of width five centered on now: indexes one, two, three, four, and five. Two of those five indexes, four and five, are strictly after the query. Two fifths of the window is future. If the width-five window is instead causal, ending at now, it uses indexes negative one through three, and index negative one does not exist, so you shorten it to zero through three, which is exactly the legal set from the mask. The centered version and the causal version are not the same average. Half of a centered window is a rough description when the center is interior and the width is odd: two future, two past, and the center, so two of the five taps are future, which is forty percent, not fifty. Say forty percent on this count rather than a slogan. Either way the future taps are illegal for a forecast. A causal mask sets their scores to negative infinity. A centered smoother does not, until you force it to.
