The board in front of you is titled a risk-free asset collapses the frontier to a ray. Mixing a risky portfolio with cash, or with borrowing at the same rate, traces a straight line from the point zero risk and mean equal to the risk-free rate, through that risky portfolio. Every mix on the line shares one risky recipe. Only the scale changes. The best risky portfolio is the one where this line is steepest. That slope is the Sharpe ratio.

Speak the ratio the way the slide writes it. S of w equals the quantity w transpose mu minus r sub f, divided by the square root of w transpose capital sigma w. The numerator is expected excess return over the risk-free rate. The denominator is standard deviation, not variance. The line is drawn in the plane whose horizontal axis is standard deviation. Slope is rise in expected return over run in standard deviation. If you divided by variance you would be scoring a different picture, and it would not be the slope of the ray you just drew.

Take a worked pair and score three risky portfolios before you optimize anything, so the slope is a number and not a compliment. Let the means be 0.08 and 0.14. Let the standard deviations be 0.20 and 0.25. Let the correlation be 0.20, so the covariance is 0.20 times 0.20 times 0.25, which is 0.01. The variances are 0.04 and 0.0625. Let the risk-free rate be 0.03. Asset 1 alone has excess return 0.08 minus 0.03, which is 0.05, and standard deviation 0.20, so its Sharpe ratio is 0.05 divided by 0.20, which is 0.25. Asset 2 alone has excess return 0.11 and standard deviation 0.25, so its Sharpe ratio is 0.11 divided by 0.25, which is 0.44. An even split has mean 0.11, excess 0.08, and variance one half squared times 0.04 plus one half squared times 0.0625 plus 2 times one half times one half times 0.01, which is 0.01 plus 0.015625 plus 0.005, which is 0.030625. The standard deviation is 0.175. The Sharpe ratio is 0.08 divided by 0.175, which is about 0.457. The even split already beats both single assets on this slope. Beating them does not make it the tangency portfolio. It makes it a candidate the ray can pass through, and a better candidate may sit nearby.

The capital market line, once you have the best risky portfolio, starts at the risk-free point and passes through tangency. Points on the segment between cash and tangency are long the fund and long cash. Points beyond tangency, above the risky bullet in the region of higher mean and higher standard deviation, require borrowing at r sub f. Say if that rate is a fiction. If you cannot borrow at the same 0.03 you used in the formula, the part of the ray beyond the tangency portfolio is a drawing of a market you do not have. The segment between cash and tangency can still describe lending at 0.03. The two sides of the point are not the same institutional fact, and the formula does not notice the difference unless you say it.

Leverage moves you along the ray. It does not change the risky mix. Suppose the tangency portfolio, which we will derive on the next board and which I will use here as a completed object, puts weight 27 over 79 on the first asset and 52 over 79 on the second. To four decimal places those weights are 0.3418 and 0.6582. Its mean is about 0.1195. Its standard deviation is about 0.1904. Its excess over 0.03 is about 0.0895, and its Sharpe ratio is about 0.470. Now put half the capital in that mix and half in the risk-free asset. The mean becomes one half of 0.1195 plus one half of 0.03, about 0.0747. The standard deviation becomes one half of 0.1904, about 0.0952. The excess return halves, the standard deviation halves, and the ratio stays about 0.470. Borrow instead. Put 1.5 times capital in the tangency mix and finance the extra half at 0.03, which is a weight of negative 0.5 on the risk-free asset. The mean is 1.5 times about 0.1195 minus 0.5 times 0.03, about 0.1642. The standard deviation is 1.5 times about 0.1904, about 0.2856. The Sharpe ratio is again about 0.470. You did not find a better risky recipe by borrowing. You slid up the same ray. If the borrowing rate is not really 0.03, this upper point is the fiction the slide told you to name.

Compare the ray with a worse fund at the same standard deviation, so steepness is not abstract. Take standard deviation 0.10 on the tangency ray. The scale y that hits that risk is 0.10 divided by about 0.1904, about 0.525. The mean is 0.03 plus 0.525 times about 0.0895, about 0.077. The same standard deviation built from asset 1 and cash uses scale 0.10 divided by 0.20, which is one half. The mean is 0.03 plus one half times 0.05, which is 0.055. Same risk, mean 0.077 against mean 0.055, because the slope 0.470 beats the slope 0.25. That gap is the whole content of choosing the tangency fund before you choose leverage.

You might ask why the denominator is standard deviation rather than variance, since the frontier you already know was built out of a variance objective. Because the picture changed axes when the risk-free asset arrived. Variance is the quadratic you minimize to trace the risky bullet. The ray is a straight line in mean and standard deviation. Its slope divides by the horizontal unit you actually plotted. A ratio that used variance would rank portfolios differently and would not equal the geometric slope of the line from the risk-free point. On the numbers, asset 2 has variance 0.0625 and excess 0.11, so excess over variance is 1.76, while its Sharpe ratio is 0.44. Asset 1 has excess over variance equal to 0.05 divided by 0.04, which is 1.25, and Sharpe ratio 0.25. The order happens to agree here. It does not have to be the definition. The definition on this board is excess over standard deviation.

Write four points on the tangency ray so the collapse to a line is a table you can recompute. Use the fund with mean about 0.1195, standard deviation about 0.1904, and risk-free rate 0.03. At scale y equal to 0 you are in cash: mean 0.03, standard deviation 0, and there is no Sharpe ratio at that single point because the denominator is zero and the excess is zero. The ray begins there. It does not have a slope at a lonely origin until a second point exists. At y equal to one half, mean about 0.0747, standard deviation about 0.0952, slope about 0.470. At y equal to 1 you are fully in the fund: mean about 0.1195, standard deviation about 0.1904, slope about 0.470. At y equal to 1.5, mean about 0.1642, standard deviation about 0.2856, slope still about 0.470. Plot mean against standard deviation and the four points sit on one line. The risky bullet, the curve of mixes that do not use cash, bends underneath that line except at the tangency point, where the line touches. That touch is why the fund is called tangency. A line through the even split would be less steep, slope about 0.457, and it would not be the supporting line. It would cut into the region the steeper line already reaches at lower risk.
